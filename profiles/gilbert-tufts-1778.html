Biography

Gilbert Tufts, second son of Daniel Tufts, the Charlestown brick-maker and Abigail Tufts, was a tanner on the Charlestown peninsula. Wyman records his birth as April 27, 1778, and his marriage to Mary Chickering on December 26, 1810 (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 968. Internet Archive).

“Gilbert Tufts was an able and successful business man. I have referred to him in a former article.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 163. Internet Archive

“Next below this estate was that of Gilbert Tufts, the father of Honorable Arthur W. Tufts, who, a few years ago, removed from this, his native place, to Boston Highlands, where he has become somewhat distinguished. Gilbert Tufts was of the firm of Nathan Tufts & Co., extensive tanners, who must be mentioned hereafter in a notice of the business of the town. Mr. Tufts sold this estate to Charles Forster, and removed into a new house on Washington Street.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 39. Internet Archive

The earlier notice is Sawyer’s account of the estates below the Abbott house on Cordis Street. Later he records that the Rev. Henry Jackson, on leaving for Hartford, sold his house on Washington Street, nearest Union Street, to Gilbert Tufts:

“On his removal to Hartford he sold his house on Washington Street to Gilbert Tufts, who removed here from Cordis Street and died July 7, 1850.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 87. Internet Archive

His cousin Joseph F. Tufts, eldest son of Deacon Amos Tufts, “carried on an extensive business as a partner with Gilbert Tufts in a tannery on Main Street, near the Neck” (Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 166. Internet Archive). Wyman notes a lot purchased of Amos Carleton in 1816 (p. 968). Children named there are Abby, who married Frederick Williams; Gilbert; Caroline, who married Dr. J. E. Bartlett and died in 1851; Sarah Scholfield; and Arthur Webster (p. 968). Mary (Chickering) Tufts died June 24, 1863, as given on a tablet (p. 968).

Wyman records his death as July 7, 1850, on a granite monument, and as the 9th in the record (p. 968). Sawyer follows the monument and gives his age as seventy-two (pp. 87, 163–164).

Director of the Bunker Hill Bank, 1831–1848

Gilbert Tufts followed his uncle Nathan Tufts onto the board of the Bunker Hill Bank in Charlestown. The bank’s list of directors shows him elected in 1831 and retiring in 1848 (Professional and Industrial History of Suffolk County, Massachusetts, vol. 2 (1894), p. 273. Internet Archive). See also Banks and savings institutions.

Service Record

Town: Charlestown, Mass. · Dates: 27 April 1778–7 July 1850

Born in 1778, he was too young for Revolutionary service. No civil office is recorded for him in the sources used here.

Family Connection

Father: Daniel Tufts (1753–1839), brick-maker. Mother: Abigail Tufts (Wyman, p. 968). Brothers: Daniel Tufts Jr. (1776–1868), Charles Tufts (1781–1876) and Nathan Tufts “2d” (1786–1872). Grandfather: Nathan Tufts, cordwainer. Uncles: Deacon Amos Tufts and Nathan Tufts of Charlestown. Wife: Mary Chickering, married December 26, 1810; died June 24, 1863. Children as named by Wyman, p. 968: Abby (m. Frederick Williams), Gilbert, Caroline (m. Dr. J. E. Bartlett; d. 1851), Sarah Scholfield, and Arthur Webster, the Honorable Arthur W. Tufts of Sawyer, p. 39.

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Daniel Tufts, brick-maker; brother Daniel Tufts Jr. (1776–1868); brother Charles Tufts (1781–1876); brother Nathan Tufts “2d” (1786–1872); grandfather Nathan Tufts, cordwainer; uncle Deacon Amos Tufts; uncle Nathan Tufts of Charlestown; cousin and partner Joseph F. Tufts (1790–1854).

Spouse(s)

  • Mary (Chickering) Tufts (Wyman’s Chickering [4]). Married December 26, 1810; died June 24, 1863, as given on a tablet (Wyman, vol. 2, p. 968).
