Biography

Hannah Tufts, a single woman of Medford, was born there on 18 March 1775 and died there on 18 May 1841 (FamilySearch Family Tree, entry for Hannah Tufts, ID KZ53-PJ5 (a user-contributed tree, not a primary source)). She inherited land from her father, Isaac Tufts, under his will of 1823. After her death her administrator, James Russell, sold the estate at auction; the highest bidder was Timothy Cotting, who later gave the land to Tufts College.

Hannah Tufts’s Land and the Cotting Gift to Tufts College

In 1937 Prof. Samuel L. Conner traced, through the Middlesex deeds, the land that the Medford baker and Tufts College trustee Timothy Cotting (d. 1872) gave to the college in 1869. Among the deeds to Cotting he found the sale of a Tufts property:

“…I came across a transfer by James Russell, the administrator of the estate of Hannah Tufts, described as a single woman, when a sale had been conducted of her estate, and Timothy Cotting mentioned as the highest bidder. The description was vague, but partially identified by the mention of a lane and a bridge over the Boston & Lowell Railroad, and a further identification of the lands as abutting the real estate venture of President John Adams. Again I ran across the statement of a map made by W. A. Mason as of March 28, 1842. The property had been willed to Hannah Tufts by her father, Isaac Tufts, as of 1823.”

Samuel L. Conner, “Timothy Cotting, Tufts College Trustee and Civic Leader of Medford,” Medford Historical Register XL, no. 2 (June 1937), p. 28. View the page scan

Cotting conveyed the land to Tufts College for “$1 and other valuable consideration.” The article’s frontispiece plan shows it on the west side of the college land, between South Street (now Winthrop Street) and the Boston & Lowell Railroad (Samuel L. Conner, “Timothy Cotting, Tufts College Trustee and Civic Leader of Medford,” Medford Historical Register XL, no. 2 (June 1937), p. 28 and frontispiece. View the page scan). The college later found itself “land poor,” and the tract was sold off and built over rather than joined to the campus.

Service Record

Town: Medford, Mass. · Dates: 18 March 1775 – 18 May 1841

No military service is recorded for her. Her father, Sgt. Isaac Tufts (1744–1823), answered the Lexington alarm and served at Dorchester Heights.

Family Connection
