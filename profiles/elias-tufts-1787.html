Biography

Elias Tufts was born in Medford on January 30, 1787, the eighth child of James Tufts, the potter, and Elizabeth (Hay) Tufts. The town record reads:

“Elias, s. James [James Jr., p.r.42.] and Elizabeth, Jan. 30, 1787.”

Vital Records of Medford, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850 (1907), p. 147. Page (Internet Archive)

Brooks gives the same date in his list of this family (Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 553. Page scan (Internet Archive)). Elias became a wheelwright. He married Abigail (Nabby) Symmes in Medford on May 23, 1813, and they had two sons, Alfred (1818–1901), wheelwright and ship joiner, and Larkin Turner (1822–1905), ship joiner, who later compiled Tufts family records. Elias died in Medford on May 18, 1868 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 39, no. 190-8; Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 271, no. 422-1, 422-2). His son followed him in the trade; the town record of a grandson’s birth in 1845 names “Alfred, wheelwright” as the father (Vital Records of Medford, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850 (1907), p. 147. Page (Internet Archive)).

The Main Street Fire of 1850

Late on the night of Thursday, November 21, 1850, fire broke out in the loft of Gregg’s stable on the north side of Main Street in Medford, near the bridge over the Mystic. Driven by a fresh northwest wind, it crossed to both sides of the street and burned until about two in the morning, destroying some twenty-five buildings between the bridge and the Medford House hotel, leaving thirty or forty families homeless, and doing about $75,000 in damage. Engine companies came from Boston, Somerville, Charlestown, Chelsea, Malden, Reading, Woburn and Cambridge. A contemporary account, printed in George P. Little’s The Fireman’s Own Book (1860), was reprinted by the Medford Historical Society in 1939 (“A Medford Disaster of 1850,” Medford Historical Register XLII, nos. 3–4 (September–December 1939), pp. 59–62).

Two Tufts wheelwrights had shops in the path of the fire, one on each side of Main Street. On the north side, where the fire began, it took the shop of Richard Tufts, a younger wheelwright of another branch of the family, and the house of “the Misses Tufts”:

“Next to the Gregg estate was Timothy Cutting’s house, blacksmith shop, and two stables, which were totally destroyed. Mr. Nathan Barker occupied a part of the dwelling-house. Mr. George Lynne’s house, blacksmith shop and stable came next, and were also destroyed. The Misses Tufts’ dwelling-house, and Richard Tufts’ wheelwright shop, on the same side of Main Street, were also totally laid in ashes.”

“A Medford Disaster of 1850,” Medford Historical Register XLII, nos. 3–4 (September–December 1939), p. 60, quoting George P. Little, The Fireman’s Own Book (1860). View the page scan

On the south side it took his own shop and house:

“On the opposite side of Main Street, the fire commenced at the bridge with the dwelling-house of Nathan W. White, and swept down Daniel Lawrence’s store and dwelling-house, James Hyde’s dwelling-house and store, Elias Tufts’ wheelwright shop and dwelling-house, George E. Willis’ tinware shop and dwelling-house, Mitchell’s barber shop and dwelling-house, Benjamin Parker’s dwelling-house and stable, Moses Merrill & Son’s paint shop and Hartshorn’s harness shop, in the same building, a ten-footer occupied by an Irish family, and three stables — were all totally destroyed.”

“A Medford Disaster of 1850,” Medford Historical Register XLII, nos. 3–4 (September–December 1939), p. 61, quoting George P. Little, The Fireman’s Own Book (1860). View the page scan

See also Businesses: The Tufts wheelwright shops on Main Street.

He is not the same man as Elias Tufts (b. 1751), who died at Pembroke, New Hampshire.

Family Connection

Father: James Tufts, potter (1755–1810). Mother: Elizabeth (Hay) Tufts (1753–1828). Grandfather: James Tufts. Brothers and sisters: Mary (Mrs. Benjamin Reed), James Jr. (1777–1825), Elizabeth (Mrs. Samuel Newhall), Lucretia (Mrs. Reuben Richardson), Mercy (1782–1855, unmarried), two Sarahs, Frances (Mrs. Luke Richardson Jr.), two Lucys, and Nancy (Mrs. Amasa J. Boynton) (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 37–39). Sons: Alfred and Larkin Turner Tufts (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 271).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father James Tufts, potter (1755–1810); grandfather James Tufts; a different man of the same name: Elias Tufts (b. 1751); fellow Medford wheelwright Richard Tufts (1804–1885), whose shop burned in the same fire; the Misses Tufts, whose Main Street house burned in 1850.

Spouse(s)

  • Abigail (Nabby) Symmes (1785–1863), daughter of Capt. John and Elizabeth (Wright) Symmes; married May 23, 1813, at Medford; died at Medford, August 11, 1863 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 39).

Birth date: the family record gives January 20, 1787, but the printed town record and Brooks both read January 30; the town record is followed here. Who the “Misses Tufts” were is not stated in the account. Richard Tufts’s unmarried sisters Hannah (1801–1864) and Emily (1806–1895) were living in Medford in 1850 and are the likeliest candidates, since their house stood on the same side of Main Street as his shop (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 257, no. 143-3, 143-5); Elias Tufts’s unmarried sister Mercy (1782–1855) is another possibility (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 38, no. 190-5).
