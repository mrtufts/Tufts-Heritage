Biography

Edward Tufts, the youngest of Amos Tufts’s seven sons, was a clerk who lived in Lowell. With his brother William and his nephews George F. and Alfred, he held the old Dexter Farm lands in South Medford left by his uncle Nathan Tufts (1764–1835). Edward Street in Medford’s Winter Hill neighborhood is one of the streets “named for members of the Tufts family” (Medford Historical Commission); which Edward it honors is not stated.

“labeled “W E G F & A Tufts,” William, Edward, George F, and Alfred Tufts. William (1802-1862) and Edward (1806-1875) were the two youngest of Amos’s seven sons. William Tufts lived in Charlestown and was a clerk and later secretary of an insurance company; Edward Tufts was also a clerk and lived in Lowell. George and Alfred were sons of Joseph Frothingham Tufts (1790-1854) the oldest of Amos’s sons. George F Tufts (1825-1920) was a civil engineer and later a bank president; Alfred Tufts (1829-1882) was a clerk, an engineer, and a bank teller.”

Medford Historical Commission, Inventory Form A, “Edward Street Area” (2022), continuation sheet 1. PDF

“This real estate Nathan Tufts bequeathed to the sons of his two brothers Daniel and Amos, and it appears that it was the sons of Amos (1762-1839) who took charge of the land in this vicinity known as Dexter Farm.”

Medford Historical Commission, Inventory Form A, “Edward Street Area” (2022), continuation sheet 1. PDF

In the 1850s and 1860s they laid out the Tufts subdivisions of South Medford (Winter Hill), labeled on the 1875 Beers atlas “W E G F & A Tufts.” See Places: The Tufts Subdivisions of South Medford.

Lowell and Chelmsford

The family record describes Edward Tufts as a clerk and paymaster in Lowell and gives his death at Chelmsford on March 26, 1875 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 232, no. 128-11). Less than a year later, the Central Congregational Church of Chelmsford was organized in his widow’s house:

“On February 12, at the residence of Mrs. Edward Tufts, the organization was effected and articles of Faith and Covenant adopted.”

Wilson Waters, History of Chelmsford, Massachusetts (1917), p. 714. Internet Archive

The meeting was held in 1876, and “Mrs. Tufts” is named again among those present (Wilson Waters, History of Chelmsford, Massachusetts (1917), p. 714. Internet Archive).

Business

Nathan Tufts (c. 1764–1835), the Charlestown butcher and tanner who bequeathed land to the sons of his brothers Daniel and Amos, built the mill pond at Charlestown Neck and ran a tide mill, tannery and wharf there. See Tide mill of Nathan Tufts.

Mill on the Medford Turnpike

In 1848 he was one of the owners of “the saw and grist mills on the turnpike” at Medford, with his brother and Gershom Cutter (Medford Historical Register, vol. XXIII, no. 1 (March 1920), “The Mills on the Medford Turnpike,” pp. 20–21. Internet Archive). See Businesses: Tufts Mills on the Medford Turnpike.

In 1848 the turnpike agent was directed “to Consult Counsell,” and later “to confer with the Messrs. Tufts in regard to damage sustained by the corporation by their neglecting to maintain their culvert,” etc. The result of this conference was a three-party agreement. The first party was the owner of the farm occupied by J. Q. Adams; the second, the turnpike company; and the third “the owners of the saw and grist mills on the turnpike,” William Tufts, Edward Tufts and Gershom Cutter. The first two and Joseph F.
