Biography

Samuel Tufts, farmer, was born at Charlestown on November 24, 1737, a son of Peter Tufts (1697–1776) and Lydia (Bucknam) Tufts. He lived on Milk Row, now Somerville Avenue near Laurel Street. On the night of April 18, 1775, he was still up, casting bullets with his slave in a hut behind the house, when the British column went quietly by. A neighbor, the widow Rand, saw the soldiers and ran across the road to tell him. At first he did not believe her. Then his lantern showed the many footprints in the dirt road, and he rode by a bridle path to Cambridge to spread the alarm (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 52–53, no. 18-5). The story is told on his brother Timothy’s profile from Ellen Chase’s account.

He later served as a private. He headed his household in the 1810 and 1820 censuses and died at Charlestown on October 24, 1828 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 53).

Service Record

Town: Milk Row, Charlestown (now Somerville), Mass. · Dates: 24 November 1737 – 24 October 1828

The DAR lists him as DAR Ancestor no. A116814: civil and patriotic service, Massachusetts. He suffered losses to the British in 1775, was town treasurer of Charlestown in 1777–1778 and 1780, and selectman in 1780. The DAR cites Hunnewell’s A Century of Town Life (vol. 9, p. 173) and the Charlestown town records. The DAR grave index gives his service as private in Capt. Isaac Hall’s company, Col. Thomas Gardner’s regiment, and his burial as the Somerville Cemetery (DAR Genealogical Research System, Revolutionary War grave and marker records).

Family Connection

Father: Peter Tufts (1697–1776). Brothers: Peter Tufts of Winter Hill (1728–1791) and Timothy Tufts (1735–1805). Children: Samuel, Martha, Lydia, Susanna, Mary, and Elizabeth (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 52).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Peter Tufts (1697–1776); brother Timothy Tufts (1735–1805); brother Peter Tufts of Winter Hill.

Spouse(s)

  • Martha Adams (b. September 25, 1746, at Menotomy; d. August 18, 1811, at Charlestown), daughter of Dea. Joseph Adams Jr. and Martha (Frost) Adams and sister of his brother Timothy’s wife; married May 11, 1769, at Menotomy (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 52).
