Biography

Amos Tufts was a Charlestown blacksmith and, for some years, the leading layman of the First Church. He was a son of Nathan Tufts, the Medford cordwainer and farmer, and a brother of Nathan Tufts of Charlestown.

“Deacon Amos Tufts lived on the corner of Main and Bow streets, in a house built about 1800. The lot of land on which it stood was formerly a part of the estate of Isaiah Edes, who sold it to N. Trask, and he to Amos Tufts in 1796.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 165. Internet Archive

Sawyer describes the house as wooden and “oblong-square,” one end on Main Street and the broadside on Bow Street, with its front door looking up Middlegate Street toward Town Hill. He lived there until his death on November 26, 1839, at seventy-seven (p. 165).

“He carried on an extensive business, for his time, as a blacksmith. His shop was in the rear of his house on Main Street, and the area between the two buildings was always a scene of industry. The shop was a brick building, three stories high, erected with the view of alteration at some time into a dwelling-house, which was afterwards done.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 165–166. Internet Archive

His eldest surviving son, Joseph F. Tufts, worked early for his uncle Nathan in the tanning business, was later a partner of Gilbert Tufts in a tannery on Main Street near the Neck, and served as treasurer of the Warren Institution for Savings. One of his daughters married Deacon Eliab P. Mackintire, a Charlestown dry-goods merchant and legislator (Sawyer, pp. 166–167). His sons William and Edward later owned the Tufts saw and grist mills on the Medford Turnpike; see Businesses.

Family Connection

Father: Nathan Tufts, cordwainer, of Medford. Mother: Mary Adams (Adams genealogy, p. 974). Brothers: Daniel Tufts, brick-maker, and Nathan Tufts (c. 1764–1835). Children on this site: William Tufts (1802–1862) and Edward Tufts (1806–1875); son Joseph F. Tufts (1790–1854); grandsons George F. and Alfred Tufts. A daughter, Mary, married Eliab P. Mackintire (Wyman, p. 969; Sawyer, pp. 166–167).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Nathan Tufts, cordwainer; brother Nathan Tufts (c. 1764–1835); son William Tufts; son Edward Tufts; grandson George F. Wyman’s Record

Wyman records Amos Tufts, son of Nathan [48], as a blacksmith of Medford and Charlestown, born July 30, 1762 (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 967. Internet Archive); married to Deborah Frothingham, who died March 24 or 27, 1843; and himself dying November 26, 1839, aged 77 (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 969. Internet Archive). Among their children he names Joseph Frothingham, born July 19, 1790; Mary, who married Eliab P. Mackintire on February 20, 1822; William, born August 25, 1802; and Edward, born July 6, 1806 (p. 969). An elder son Amos, born July 5, 1787, died July 10, 1800, “caught his death by going Maying.”
