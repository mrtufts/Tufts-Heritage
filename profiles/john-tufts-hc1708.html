Biography

John Tufts was born in Medford, a son of Capt. Peter Tufts, and graduated from Harvard College in 1708. He became minister of the parish in the western part of Newbury, now West Newbury, and is remembered as the author of one of the first singing-books printed in the colonies.

“A year previous to Mr. Belcher’s death, John Tufts was ordained. He was born in Medford, graduated at Harvard in 1708. He preached twenty-four years, when he was dismissed by a council called on complaint of some disaffected women of the parish. Mr. Tufts was the author of the first singing-book used in the colony, previous to the publication of which many churches had but two or three tunes, and less than a dozen in all, York, Windsor, and Martyrs, being the best known.”

Standard History of Essex County, Massachusetts (1878), p. 423. Internet Archive (Scanned text; obvious scanning errors corrected.)

He was in demand as a preacher beyond his own parish. At the ordination of the Rev. Mr. Bradstreet over the new parish at Squam (Annisquam), “The sermon at the ordination was preached by Rev. John Tufts, of Newbury, and was published” (Standard History of Essex County, Massachusetts (1878), p. 138. Internet Archive).

Family Connection

Father: Capt. Peter Tufts (c. 1648–1721), whose sons who lived included the Rev. Thomas Tufts (Harvard 1701), the Rev. John Tufts of Newbury (Harvard 1708), and Dr. Simon Tufts (1700–1747) (NEHGR 51, pp. 302–303, as cited on his father’s page).

8. John3 (Peter2 Peter1), Harvard College 1708 ; minister, settled over second church of Newbury, 30 June, 1714; resigned 1738; shop keeper at Amesbury. He wrote as early as 1714 “An Introduction to the Singing of Psalm-Tunes, In a plain and easy Method, with A Collection of Tunes. In Three Parts.” It advocated singing by note, and was the first book of the kind published in New England. It passed through several editions.
