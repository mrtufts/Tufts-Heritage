Biography

Aaron Tufts of Dudley, styled Hon. and sometimes Dr. in later compilations, was a woolen manufacturer and a justice of the peace. He was one of the original corporators of the Merino Wool Factory, with Learned Corbin, Jeptha Bacon, Phineas Bemis and William Learned, incorporated 13 February 1812 (D. Hamilton Hurd, ed., History of Worcester County, Massachusetts, with Biographical Sketches of Many of Its Pioneers and Prominent Men (Philadelphia: J. W. Lewis, 1889), the Dudley chapter (transcribed from that volume; printed page not confirmed)). In 1816 he and partners, among them John R. Jewett, Mayo Pratt, Harvey Conant and William Robinson, built a woolen mill in the north part of the town, on a privilege supplied from Gore and Baker Ponds, a place later called Ram’s Horn or Tufts Village. A stone mill replaced the earlier frame building in 1827; in 1832 it was reported to have made 40,000 yards of broadcloth (Massachusetts Historical Commission, Reconnaissance Survey Town Report: Dudley, 1984).

The family record gives his birth as 30 January 1770 at Charlestown, a son of Aaron Tufts (1739–1773) and Mary (Stone) Tufts, his marriage to Sarah (Sally) Barker at Worcester on 23 May 1790, and his death at Dudley on 17 October 1844 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 112, no. 56-1, citing the Massachusetts vital records, vol. 9, p. 124). It notes service in the War of 1812, as a guard at the State Arsenal, and that he was an original stockholder of the Manufacturers’ and Merchants’ Bank in Boston in 1814, before he built up the community called Tuftsville. Sarah died at Dudley on 2 March 1842 (Dudley vital records, p. 283, as cited there). One son, George A., is recorded.

He is not Aaron Tufts of Medford (1761–1781), who died long before this mill was built, and not Aaron Tufts (1793–1881) of Pavilion, New York.

Service Record

Town: Charlestown (Somerville), Mass.; Dudley, Mass. · Dates: 30 Jan. 1770 – 17 Oct. 1844

Civil office: justice of the peace for Worcester County (the 1825 writ on this page). The family record notes service in the War of 1812 as a guard at the State Arsenal (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 112). No Revolutionary service is expected; he was a child in 1775.

Family Connection

Parents: Aaron Tufts (16 Dec. 1739 – 29 Oct. 1773) of Charlestown, and Mary Stone (22 Nov. 1747 – 22 Oct. 1824), daughter of Lieut. Moses and Hannah (Tainter) Stone of Watertown; she married, second, Capt. William Lyon of Woodstock, Conn. (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, no. 18-6 and p. 112). Wife: Sarah Barker. Child: George A.

Relatives with profiles on this site: a different man: Aaron Tufts of Medford (1761–1781); a different man: Aaron Tufts (1793–1881) of Pavilion, N.Y..

Spouse(s)
