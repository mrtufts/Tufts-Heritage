Biography

Nathan Tufts was a shoemaker by trade who made his living mostly by farming in Medford. He was a son of Peter Tufts of the Milk Row road in Charlestown (now Somerville), and his three sons became leading business men of Charlestown: a brick-maker, a blacksmith, and a butcher, tanner and mill owner.

“One of the sons of this Peter was Nathan Tufts, by trade a cordwainer, but chiefly occupied as a farmer in Medford. He was the father of Daniel, Deacon Amos, and Nathan Tufts, who for the greater part of their lives were prominent citizens of the old town and among its leading business men.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 162. Internet Archive

His sons on this site are Deacon Amos Tufts (c. 1762–1839) and Nathan Tufts of Charlestown (c. 1764–1835). Sawyer describes the eldest, Daniel, as a brick-maker and farmer who lived outside Charlestown Neck and sat in the Legislature in 1811 and 1812 (p. 162).

Birth, Marriage and Death

The family record gives his birth as May 14, 1724, at Charlestown (now Somerville), and his death as December 21, 1771, at Medford; his widow Mary married Richard Clark of Watertown in 1777 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 50, no. 18-1). The Arlington register adds the marriage: “Nathan of Chas., bro. of Peter (1), m. Mary Adams of Camb. 6 June 1751” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 310. Internet Archive). He is a different man from Nathan Tufts (1741–1784), who was nine years old at that marriage.

Family Connection

Father: Peter Tufts of Milk Row, carpenter and farmer (Sawyer, p. 162). Wife: Mary Adams, as named in the Adams genealogy (p. 974) as the mother of his sons Amos and Nathan. Sons: Daniel (brick-maker, of Charlestown), Deacon Amos, and Nathan.

Tufts (1790–1854); a different man: Nathan Tufts (1741–1784).

Wyman’s Record

Wyman records Nathan Tufts, cordwainer, son of Peter Tufts [36], as marrying Mary Adams on June 6, 1751; he died December 21, 1771, and administration was granted to his widow on January 14, 1772. She married second Richard Clark of Watertown on November 25, 1777. Children: Nathan (b. 7 June 1752, d. 5 Aug. 1762); Daniel (b. 27 Dec. 1753); Susanna (b. 31 Jan. 1756, m. Jonathan Foster); Mary (b. 17 Mar. 1758, m. Seth Stone); Abigail (b. 20 Jan. 1760, d. 1777); Amos (b. 30 July 1762); Nathan (b. 23 Mar. 1764) (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 967. Internet Archive). Because he died in 1771, he is not the Nathan Tufts who died in 1784.
