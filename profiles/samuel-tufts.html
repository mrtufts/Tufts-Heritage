Biography

Many records name a Samuel Tufts, most of them from Medford. Medford itself told its Samuels apart as Samuel, Samuel Jr., and Samuel 3d. Each record appears separately below. Matches with Wild's biographical notes are given only where the details agree closely.

An Incorporator of the Middlesex Canal, 1793

The Middlesex Canal, which linked the Merrimack River at Chelmsford with the Medford River and Boston, received its charter from the General Court, “signed by Governor Hancock on the 22d of June, 1793” (Wilson Waters, History of Chelmsford, Massachusetts (1917), p. 503. Internet Archive). The act named the first proprietors:

“Be it therefore enacted, by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, that the said James Sullivan, Oliver Prescott, Jas. Winthrop, Loammi Baldwin, Benjamin Hall, Jonathan Porter, Andrew Hall, Ebenezer Hall, Samuel Tufts, Jr., Aaron Brown, Willis Hall, Samuel Swan, Jr., and Ebenezer Hall, Jr., their associates and successors, are hereby incorporated and shall be a corporation forever, under the name of The Proprietors of the Middlesex Canal”

Wilson Waters, History of Chelmsford, Massachusetts (1917), p. 506. Internet Archive

Several of the names, the Halls, Jonathan Porter and Samuel Swan Jr., belong to Medford families, and the canal crossed the Brooks estate at Medford on its way to the Charlestown mill-pond (Wilson Waters, History of Chelmsford, Massachusetts (1917), p. 506. Internet Archive).

⚑ Unverified

Which Samuel Tufts Jr. The act gives no town or dates. He was probably of Medford, like several of his fellow incorporators, but which of the Medford Samuels listed on this page he was has not been established. Note: Pending further work.

From Tufts Kinsmen

Vol. II traces several separate Samuel5 lines. These include “Samuel5, William4, James3, Jonathan2, Peter1” (p. 96), “Samuel5, Joseph4, Joseph3, Jonathan2, Peter1” (pp. 107–111), and “Samuel5, Samuel4, Joseph3, Jonathan2, Peter1” (p. 114). (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II)

Possible matches only. Wild’s Samuel 3d was the son of William, his Samuel b. 1732 the son of Joseph, and his Samuel b. 1761 the son of Samuel. These fit the three lines above in parentage, but Vol. II gives no service or dates for any Samuel5. None is attached to a specific MSS entry.

A Notice to the Newburyport Selectmen, 1766

A short handwritten note on laid paper, about 6¼ by 8 inches, was addressed to the selectmen of Newburyport in July 1766. In it Samuel Tufts tells them that on July 7 he had taken into his family Samuel Resnick, aged about eight, the son of Isaac Resnick, late of Newbury, deceased. The note is dated July 11, 1766, and signed “Saml Tufts.” The back is docketed “Notification from S Tufts.”

The paper reads as a town notice of a child taken into a household, of a kind consistent with colonial Massachusetts poor-law practice, by which a town was informed when a child was received into a family. That reading is offered with caution; the note itself does not name the legal arrangement.

A later commercial description of the manuscript gave the month as February, but the handwriting reads July, which is followed here.

Gentlemen / This is to Notify you that I / Have this Seventh day of July 1766 taken / into my family to live with me Saml / Resnick aged about Eight years old — / son of Isaac Resnick Late of Newby / Deceasd — Dated this 11th day of July — / 1766 — Saml Tufts / To / The Gentlemen / Select men / for the Town / of Newbury Port

Transcription from the photographs of the manuscript, with the original line breaks marked by slashes. The photographs are from eBay item 237179159588, listed by Antique Cottage Collectibles; the listing ended November 17, 2026, with no bids. eBay listing

The writer was most likely Dr. Samuel Tufts (1735–1799), physician and merchant, who married at Newbury in 1762, lived in Newburyport, and later served as one of its selectmen. In January 1762 a Samuel Tufts was also appointed guardian of a Newbury boy, Samuel Newhall, whose mother was Dr. Samuel’s first cousin (The Newhall Family of Lynn, Massachusetts (1882), p. 179. Internet Archive).

⚑ Unverified

The note is signed only “Saml Tufts,” and no source states that Dr. Samuel wrote it; the attribution rests on his residence, his public role in Newburyport, and the family connection. Note: Pending further work.
