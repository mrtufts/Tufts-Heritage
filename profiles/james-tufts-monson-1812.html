Biography

James Browning Tufts, who used only the name James Tufts, was born at Wardsborough, Vermont, on November 3, 1812, a son of James and Submit F. (Hayden) Tufts. A clergyman and teacher, he spent most of his life at Monson, Massachusetts, where he died on March 29, 1901. The family record notes that he was the tutor of the poet Eugene Field (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 30, no. 182-4).

Monson Academy and the Town’s Schools

In 1855 the Massachusetts Register lists “Monson Academy, Monson. Incorporated 1804. James Tufts, A. M., Principal and Teacher of the Classical Department” (The Massachusetts Register for 1855, p. 195. Internet Archive). The Hampden County history names him among the academy’s most prominent teachers, with Richard S. Storrs Jr. and the Rev. Charles Hammond (Alfred M. Copeland, ed., “Our County and Its People”: A History of Hampden County, Massachusetts, vol. 3 (1902), p. 290. Internet Archive), and credits him with a long campaign for the public schools:

“Rev. James Tufts took up the work where Mr. Hammond laid it down, and for over forty years he labored untiringly for the improvement of the school system. His last efforts were towards forming a superintendency district.”

Alfred M. Copeland, ed., “Our County and Its People”: A History of Hampden County, Massachusetts, vol. 3 (1902), p. 287. Internet Archive

He also taught privately. William Trowbridge Forbes of Westborough, born in 1850, “completed his preparation for college at the classical school of the Allens at West Newton and at the private school of Rev. James Tufts, of Monson,” before entering Amherst College (Heman Packard DeForest and Edward Craig Bates, The History of Westborough, Massachusetts (1891), p. 451. Internet Archive). In March 1877 he was one of the townsmen who began the movement for a free public library in Monson (Alfred M. Copeland, ed., “Our County and Its People”: A History of Hampden County, Massachusetts, vol. 3 (1902), p. 295. Internet Archive).

Service Record

Town: Wardsborough, Vt.; Monson, Mass. · Dates: 3 November 1812 – 29 March 1901

No military service is recorded for him.

Family Connection

Parents: James Tufts and Submit F. (Hayden) Tufts of Wardsborough, Vermont. Children: James Frederick (1860, died in infancy) and James Hayden Tufts (1862–1942), born at Monson, philosopher and educator (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 30, 260, no. 182-4; 407-2). A “James H. Tufts, Ph. D., Chicago” was a trustee of Monson Academy in 1902 (Alfred M. Copeland, ed., “Our County and Its People”: A History of Hampden County, Massachusetts, vol. 3 (1902), p. 291. Internet Archive).

Spouse(s)

  • Mary Elizabeth Warren (b. August 9, 1823, at Wardsborough, Vt.; d. November 1, 1910, at Monson), daughter of Dr. John Parker and Lucy Maynard (Wheelock) Warren; married March 21, 1855, at Brooklyn, N.Y. (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. II, p. 30).
