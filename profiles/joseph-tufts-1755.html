Biography

Joseph Tufts Jr. was born on 17 February 1755, the son of Capt. Joseph Tufts and Hannah (Cutter) Tufts of Medford. At twenty-one he helped pay the bounties that sent Medford men to Canada in the summer of 1776:

“Tufts, Joseph, Jr. Loaned money for bounty, July, 1776. Born Feb. 17, 1755; son of Joseph and Hannah; taxed in Medford, 1775 to 1794.”

Helen Tilden Wild, Medford in the Revolution (1903), p. 62. Internet Archive · page scan

“Tufts, Joseph, Jr. Account of money paid by persons to hire men to go to Canada [year not given], examined and allowed by a committee at Medford Oct. 8, 1776.”

Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (1907), p. 137. Internet Archive · page scan

His father, on the same page of Wild, served on Medford’s committees of correspondence and safety and died in December 1778. Joseph Jr. was taxed in Medford until 1794.

Later Life in Danvers

The family record identifies him as no. 43-1, the eldest son of Capt. Joseph Tufts and Hannah (Cutter) Tufts. It records that he was a private in the Revolution and lent bounty money in 1776, married his cousin Esther Dickson at Menotomy on December 23, 1779, was head of a household in Danvers in the 1820 census, and died at Danvers on March 10, 1840 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 90–91, no. 43-1). The Arlington register confirms the marriage: “Joseph Tufts of Medford m. Esther Dickson 23 Dec. 1779” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 312. Internet Archive).

Their children were Joseph, Esther, Rebecca, Lucy, Cotton, Anna, and Frederick A. (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 91). His brother Andrew Tufts settled at Worcester.

In the DAR Records

The DAR lists him as DAR Ancestor no. A116810 (“Joseph Tufts Jr.”): patriotic service, Massachusetts, “loaned money during the Revolution”; born February 17, 1755, at Medford.

⚑ Unverified

Death: the DAR gives “about 1794, probably Danvers.” The family record gives March 10, 1840, at Danvers (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 90–91, no. 43-1), and Wild says he was taxed in Medford until 1794, which may be the source of the DAR’s date. Note: Pending further work.

Service Record

Town: Medford; later Danvers, Mass. · Dates: 17 February 1755 – 10 March 1840

Patriotic service: loaned money for the Medford bounty of July 1776 (Wild, p. 62); account allowed by the Medford committee, Oct. 8, 1776 (MSS, vol. XVI, p. 137).

Family Connection

Father: Capt. Joseph Tufts Jr. (1730/1–1778). Mother: Hannah (Cutter) Tufts. The family record names his siblings as Samuel 3rd, Ammi Ruhamah, Walter, Cotton, Hannah and Andrew Tufts (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 91, no. 43-3, per the family record).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Capt. Joseph Tufts; brother Andrew Tufts.

Spouse(s)

  • Esther Dickson (1757–1848), daughter of Walter and Anne (Cutter) Dickson; married December 23, 1779, at Menotomy (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 312. Internet Archive; Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 91).
