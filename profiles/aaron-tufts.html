Aaron Tufts of Medford enlisted for three years in the Continental Army in 1777. He served in Capt. James Bancroft’s company of Col. Michael Jackson’s regiment from March 1777 to February 1780, and went out again with Medford’s six-months men in 1780. The family record gives his birth as 18 December 1761, a son of William Tufts Jr. and Susanna (Dix) Tufts, and says that he never married.

The family record and Wild’s 1903 history of Medford say he was later of the crew of the Salem privateer Essex, captured in 1781, and that he died that year a prisoner of war in Old Mill Prison at Plymouth, England.

Biography

Aaron Tufts of Medford enlisted for three years in the Continental Army in 1777. He served in Capt. James Bancroft’s company of Col. Michael Jackson’s regiment from March 1777 to February 1780, then went out again with Medford’s six-months men in 1780.

Sailor, Prisoner of War: The Navy and Essex Record

The family record adds a second chapter to Aaron’s service. Tufts Kinsmen (Vol. I, p. 177, no. 93-4) records that Aaron Tufts was born on 18 December 1761 in Medford, a son of William Tufts Jr. and Susanna (Dix) Tufts, and a member of Tufts Family 93, with a sister Eleanor (93-3) and a brother William (93-5). He never married.

Source: Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 177, no. 93-4 (the book cites MMCC 1:76 and 2:80, and GAZ 9/18/1781)

Helen Tilden Wild, writing in 1903, had already connected the young Medford soldier with that death abroad:

“Tufts, Aaron. Enlisted for 3 yrs., return dated Feb., 1778; enlisted for 8 mos., 1780; age 19. Son of William and Susanna; born Dec. 18, 1761. Probably Aaron, who died in Old Mill Prison, England, Sept. 18, 1781.”

Helen Tilden Wild, Medford in the Revolution (1903), p. 50. Internet Archive · page scan

The Essex was a Salem privateer of twenty guns commanded by Capt. John Cathcart. She sailed in April 1781 and was taken at sea on 10 June 1781 by the London privateer Queen Charlotte; her crew were committed to Mill Prison at Plymouth in July (“From Job Field and Others, 8 September 1781,” editorial note 1, Papers of John Adams, vol. 11. Massachusetts Historical Society; “Thomas Vinton to John Adams, 20 December 1781,” Papers of John Adams, vol. 12. Founders Online). If Wild’s date is right, Aaron died there on 18 September 1781, aged nineteen. The full story of the ship and the prison is on the privateer Essex and Mill Prison.

⚑ Unverified

Army or Navy? Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors (vol. XVI, p. 131, quoted above) records an Aaron Tufts of Medford in the Continental Army: Capt. Bancroft’s company, Col. Jackson’s regiment, for three years from 1777, then a six-months man from July 4 to Dec. 12, 1780. Tufts Kinsmen (Vol. I, p. 177) reads the 1778 three-year return and the 1780 eight-month enlistment as service in the new U.S. Navy, and Wild (p. 50) gives the same enlistments without naming either service. The Army discharge of December 1780 would still allow him to sail on the Essex in April 1781, but no crew list naming him has been seen. Year aboard: the family record places him on the Essex in 1780; the voyage on which she was captured began in April 1781. Captor: some accounts name HMS Roebuck (1780); the Adams Papers and a prisoner’s own letter name the privateer Queen Charlotte (10 June 1781). Date of death: 18 September 1781 is Wild’s, offered as “probably”; the family record gives 1781 and cites a gazette of 9/18/1781. Note: Pending further work.

Service Record

Town: Medford · Dates: 18 Dec. 1761 – 1781

“Tufts, Aaron, Medford. List of men raised to serve in the Continental Army from 1st Middlesex Co. regt., as returned by Lieut. Stephen Hall; residence, Medford; engaged for town of Medford; joined Capt. Bancroft's co., Col. Jackson's regt.; term, 3 years”

Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (1907), p. 131

“marched to camp July 4, 1780; discharged Dec. 12, 1780; service, 5 mos. 20 days”

Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (1907), p. 131

Later service at sea: enlisted for three years (return dated February 1778) and again for eight months in 1780, at age 19; one of the crew of the Salem privateer Essex, captured in 1781; died a prisoner of war in Old Mill Prison, Plymouth, England, in 1781 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 177, no. 93-4; Helen Tilden Wild, Medford in the Revolution (1903), p. 50. Internet Archive). See the privateer Essex and Mill Prison.

From Cutter’s Memoirs (1910)

Cutter names two Aarons in this family: a son of Lieut. William Tufts (no dates), and a grandson, born December 18, 1761, son of William Tufts Jr. and Susannah (vol. II, p. 1199).
