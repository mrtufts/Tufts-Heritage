Biography

William Tufts was one of the seven sons of Amos Tufts (1762–1839) of Charlestown, a son of Nathan and Mary (Adams) Tufts. With his brother Edward and his nephews George F. and Alfred, he took charge of the Medford land left to Amos’s sons by their uncle Nathan Tufts (1764–1835), the Charlestown butcher and tanner.

“labeled “W E G F & A Tufts,” William, Edward, George F, and Alfred Tufts. William (1802-1862) and Edward (1806-1875) were the two youngest of Amos’s seven sons. William Tufts lived in Charlestown and was a clerk and later secretary of an insurance company; Edward Tufts was also a clerk and lived in Lowell. George and Alfred were sons of Joseph Frothingham Tufts (1790-1854) the oldest of Amos’s sons. George F Tufts (1825-1920) was a civil engineer and later a bank president; Alfred Tufts (1829-1882) was a clerk, an engineer, and a bank teller.”

Medford Historical Commission, Inventory Form A, “Edward Street Area” (2022), continuation sheet 1. PDF

“This real estate Nathan Tufts bequeathed to the sons of his two brothers Daniel and Amos, and it appears that it was the sons of Amos (1762-1839) who took charge of the land in this vicinity known as Dexter Farm.”

Medford Historical Commission, Inventory Form A, “Edward Street Area” (2022), continuation sheet 1. PDF

In the 1850s and 1860s they laid out the Tufts subdivisions of South Medford (Winter Hill), labeled on the 1875 Beers atlas “W E G F & A Tufts.” See Places: The Tufts Subdivisions of South Medford.

Chief Clerk at the State House and Insurance Secretary

The family record calls William Tufts an insurance broker and notes that he was secretary of the Massachusetts Mutual Insurance Company and lived on Washington Street (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 232, no. 128-9). The annual Massachusetts Register shows a William Tufts holding that post for at least sixteen years, and also serving in the office of the Secretary of the Commonwealth:

  • 1839: under the Secretary of the Commonwealth, John P. Bigelow, “Chief Clerk, William Tufts” (The Massachusetts Register for 1839, p. 32. Internet Archive); a justice of the peace in Boston (The Massachusetts Register for 1839, p. 44. Internet Archive); and “Secretary, William Tufts” of the Massachusetts Mutual Fire Insurance Company, 17 State Street (The Massachusetts Register for 1839, p. 243. Internet Archive).
  • 1844: “Chief Clerk, Wm. Tufts,” under Secretary John G. Palfrey (The Massachusetts Register and United States Calendar for 1844, “Civil Government of Massachusetts”. Internet Archive); a Suffolk justice of the peace (The Massachusetts Register and United States Calendar for 1844, p. 43. Internet Archive); and secretary of the Massachusetts Mutual Fire Insurance Company, now at 31 State Street (The Massachusetts Register and United States Calendar for 1844, p. 244. Internet Archive).
  • 1855: “First Clerk Secretary of State’s Office. William Tufts, Salary, $1,300” (The Massachusetts Register for 1855, p. 10. Internet Archive), and secretary of the insurance company at 31 State Street (The Massachusetts Register for 1855, p. 154. Internet Archive).

Business

Nathan Tufts (c. 1764–1835), the Charlestown butcher and tanner who bequeathed land to the sons of his brothers Daniel and Amos, built the mill pond at Charlestown Neck and ran a tide mill, tannery and wharf there. See Tide mill of Nathan Tufts.

Mill on the Medford Turnpike

In 1848 he was one of the owners of “the saw and grist mills on the turnpike” at Medford, with his brother and Gershom Cutter (Medford Historical Register, vol. XXIII, no. 1 (March 1920), “The Mills on the Medford Turnpike,” pp. 20–21. Internet Archive). See Businesses: Tufts Mills on the Medford Turnpike.

In 1848 the turnpike agent was directed “to Consult Counsell,” and later “to confer with the Messrs. Tufts in regard to damage sustained by the corporation by their neglecting to maintain their culvert,” etc. The result of this conference was a three-party agreement. The first party was the owner of the farm occupied by J. Q. Adams; the second, the turnpike company; and the third “the owners of the saw and grist mills on the turnpike,” William Tufts, Edward Tufts and Gershom Cutter. The first two and Joseph F. Tufts were the farm owners, and James O. Curtis, treasurer, represented the turnpike, which for a similar consideration of stone and gravel, agreed that the mill owners …
