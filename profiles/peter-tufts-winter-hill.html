Peter Tufts, called “of Winter Hill,” was born on April 24, 1728, the second son of Peter Tufts of Milk Row and Lydia Bucknam, and was established on a farm on Winter Hill. On the night of April 18–19, 1775, the alarm came to his door; the day was also his twenty-fifth wedding anniversary. The family list of Revolutionary service names him a Medford minuteman of Winter Hill; no muster roll naming this Peter has been matched. He died on March 4, 1791.

Summary drawn from the sources cited on this page.

Biography

Peter Tufts, called “of Winter Hill” and in Jones “of Charlestown,” was born on 24 April 1728, the second son of Peter Tufts (1697–1776), “Peter of Milk Row,” and Lydia Bucknam. Booth writes that he “was established on a farm on Winter hill” (Historic Leaves, vol. 1, no. 1, pp. 21–22).

“This Peter the third is called “of Charlestown,” and was born April 24, 1728. He married Anne Adams of Quincy, a relative of President John Adams.”

Alvin Lincoln Jones, Under Colonial Roofs (1894), p. 129 · page scan

On the night the war began, the alarm came to his door:

“At the outbreak of the Revolution, Peter Tufts lived at the top of Winter Hill in a little old house, which stood until a few years ago. Here he was soundly sleeping when Paul Revere came thundering up to the door, calling on the people to rouse and arm themselves, for the Regulars were coming.”

Alvin Lincoln Jones, Under Colonial Roofs (1894), p. 129 · page scan

April 19, 1775 was also his twenty-fifth wedding anniversary. His wife, Anne Adams Tufts, nursed the wounded after Bunker Hill; the marker above stands on the door-stone of her home. Wyman records his death:

“PETER 37. Son of Peter 36; m. ANN ADAMS [18] April 19, 1750, who d. Feb. 7, 1813, æ. 84, g. s. … d. March 4, 1791, æ. 63, g. s.”

Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 965. Internet Archive · page scan

The earlier account of this family is on The Colonial Era: Peter Tufts of Winter Hill, and the farm on Places: Winter Hill.

Charlestown Selectman on the Eve of the Revolution

Frothingham’s History of Charlestown lists “Peter Tufts, Jr.” among the town’s selectmen in each year from 1771 through 1775 (Richard Frothingham Jr., The History of Charlestown, Massachusetts (1845), pp. 284–285, 308. Internet Archive). In 1774, when the Boston Port Bill closed Boston harbor, Charlestown raised gifts for the relief of Boston, and he served on the town’s committee on donations:

“The committee on donations consisted of Nathaniel Gorham, Nathaniel Frothingham, Nehemiah Rand, Peter Tufts, Jun., John Stanton, Stephen Miller, James Gardner …”

Richard Frothingham Jr., The History of Charlestown, Massachusetts (1845), p. 310. Internet Archive (Transcribed from the printed page.)

⚑ Unverified

Identity. Frothingham writes “Peter Tufts, Jr.” without dates. In the 1770s the elder Peter of Charlestown (1697–1776) was still living, so “Jr.” most likely means his son, Peter of Winter Hill (1728–1791). This is an inference from the dates, not a statement in the source. Note: Pending further work.

In the DAR Records

The DAR lists him as DAR Ancestor no. A116811 (“Peter Tufts Jr.”): civil and patriotic service, Massachusetts. He suffered losses at the burning of Charlestown and was a selectman in 1776. The record gives his baptism as April 28, 1728, at Cambridge, and his death as March 4, 1791.

His slate and his wife’s stand side by side in the Phipps Street Burying Ground, Charlestown (photograph above).

Place of death: the DAR record says Medford; Find a Grave places his death at Charlestown. Note: Pending further work.

Service Record

Town: Charlestown (Winter Hill, now Somerville), Mass. · Dates: 24 April 1728 – 4 March 1791

The family list of Revolutionary service names him “Medford Minute Man – Winter Hill.” The printed source that places him in the story of April 19, 1775 is Jones (p. 129), quoted above. The Medford roll of Capt. Isaac Hall’s company (Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (1907), p. 137–138. Internet Archive) names “Peter, Jr., Medford”; this site assigns that entry to his son Peter Tufts Jr. (1753–1832), whom Wild identifies as the minuteman.

Family Connection

Father: Peter Tufts (1697–1776), housewright, of Milk Row. Mother: Lydia Bucknam (d. Oct. 31, 1776). Brothers: Nathan (b. 1724) and Timothy Tufts (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 964. Internet Archive). Wife: Anne Adams (married April 19, 1750). Children on this site: Peter Tufts Jr., John Tufts, Ann Tufts Richardson (b. 1757), Elizabeth Tufts Swan (b. 1758), Joseph Tufts (b. 1760), Lydia Tufts Gray (b. 1762), Asa Tufts (1764–1828), Thomas Tufts of Lexington (1766–1830), Lucy Tufts Osgood (b. 1767), Rebecca Tufts Adams (1769–c. 1848), Sarah Tufts Adams. Tufts Kinsmen (Vol. I, p. 50) lists twelve children: Peter, Peter, John, Anna, Elizabeth, Joseph, Lydia, Asa, Thomas, Lucy, Rebecca and Sarah.

Spouse(s)

  • Anne (Adams) Tufts (1729–1813). Married April 19, 1750; she died Feb. 7, 1813, aged 84 (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 965. Internet Archive); Jones gives February 17, 1813 (p. 129).
