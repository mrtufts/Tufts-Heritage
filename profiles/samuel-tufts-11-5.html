Biography

Samuel Tufts was born in Medford on January 7, 1734/5, a son of Dr. Simon Tufts and Abigail (Smith) Tufts and a younger brother of Dr. Simon Tufts and Dr. Cotton Tufts. Like them he became a physician; he was also a druggist and merchant. He settled in Newbury, married Sarah Moody there on November 8, 1762, and was chosen a clerk of the market in 1764. He was one of the petitioners to set off Newburyport as a town, served as one of its selectmen from 1777 to 1783, and died at Newburyport on September 20, 1799 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 38–39, no. 11-5).

Early in 1762 a Samuel Tufts was acting in Newbury family business. When Capt. Joseph Newhall of Newbury died intestate, “guardianship of his son Samuel Newhall (above fourteen years of age) was granted 23 Jan., 1762, to Samuel Tufts” (The Newhall Family of Lynn, Massachusetts (1882), p. 179. Internet Archive). The widow, Mercy (Bradshaw) Newhall, was a daughter of John and Mercy (Tufts) Bradshaw of Medford and so Dr. Samuel’s first cousin.

A notice of July 1766 to the selectmen of Newburyport, signed “Saml Tufts,” is shown on Samuel Tufts (several men).

The family record notes that the Tufts arms appear on his bookplate (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. xxx); see Coat of Arms.

Family Connection

Father: Dr. Simon Tufts (1700–1747). Mother: Abigail (Smith) Tufts. Brothers: Dr. Simon Tufts (1727–1786), Dr. Cotton Tufts (1732–1815). Children, born at Newburyport from 1763: Sarah, Samuel, Dorothy, Simon, Sarah, and Polly (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 38).

The 1762 guardianship and the 1766 notice name only “Samuel Tufts.” Dr. Samuel is the only Samuel Tufts known to have been living in Newbury then, and he was a cousin of the Newhall widow, so both are attributed to him here as likely, not proven. Newburyport was set off from Newbury in 1764, so a 1766 notice to its selectmen fits his residence. Note: Pending further work.
