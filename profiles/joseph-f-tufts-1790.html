Biography

Joseph Frothingham Tufts, called Joseph F. Tufts by Sawyer, was the eldest surviving son of Deacon Amos Tufts and Deborah (Frothingham) Tufts. Wyman records his birth as July 19, 1790 (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 969. Internet Archive).

“Joseph F. Tufts, the eldest son of the deacon, was the father of the present treasurer of the Warren Institution for Savings — a position which he himself filled for several years. Joseph was a man of much ability, a very systematic and excellent business man, and he held many important trusts. He was employed early in life by his uncle, Nathan Tufts, in his business of tanning, and he afterwards carried on an extensive business as a partner with Gilbert Tufts in a tannery on Main Street, near the Neck. He was interested in the schools of the town, and was for some years a member of the school committee. He was also an active and leading member of the Winthrop Church and Society.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 166–167. Internet Archive

The uncle is Nathan Tufts of Charlestown (c. 1764–1835), the butcher and tanner; the partner is his cousin Gilbert Tufts. Their tannery is noted on Businesses: Tanneries on Main Street, Charlestown. In 1848 he was also named, with his brothers William and Edward, as an owner of farm land beside the Tufts mills on the Medford Turnpike (see Tufts Mills on the Medford Turnpike).

Wyman styles him a tanner and an “agent in various trusts,” and records that his marriage to Hannah Whitney, daughter of Nathaniel and Abigail Whitney of Watertown, was published March 12, 1815. She died August 15, 1872, aged eighty-one years, one month and ten days. He died in 1854, on the 17th of the month, aged 64; the month is unclear in the copy consulted (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 969. Internet Archive). The Medford Historical Commission form already cited on this site gives the years 1790–1854.

Wyman names five children: James B., born January 14, 1817; Joseph Frothingham, born November 11, 1819; Henry, born October 5, 1822; George F., born October 19 (the year is not fully legible in the original); and Alfred, born August 14 (the year likewise unclear) (p. 969). The Commission form identifies George F. Tufts (1825–1920), a civil engineer and later a bank president, and Alfred Tufts (1829–1882), clerk, engineer, bank teller and surveyor of the family’s Medford subdivision plans, as two of those sons (Edward Street Area form, 2022, continuation sheet 1). Sawyer, writing later, calls Joseph the father of “the present treasurer of the Warren Institution for Savings.”

Treasurer of the Warren Institution for Savings

The Suffolk County history gives the years of his treasurership: Henry L. Jacques “was succeeded by Joseph F. Tufts, who held the office in 1851, ’52, ’53 and ’54” (Professional and Industrial History of Suffolk County, Massachusetts, vol. 2 (1894), p. 412. Internet Archive). The same page records that “In 1865 George F. Tufts was elected treasurer, and he still occupies that position” in 1894.

Service Record

Town: Charlestown, Mass. · Dates: 19 July 1790–1854

Born in 1790, he was too young for Revolutionary service. Civil offices recorded by Sawyer are the treasurership of the Warren Institution for Savings and a term on the Charlestown school committee (pp. 166–167).

Family Connection

Father: Deacon Amos Tufts (1762–1839). Mother: Deborah (Frothingham) Tufts, who died March 24 or 27, 1843 (Wyman, p. 969). Grandfather: Nathan Tufts, cordwainer. Uncles: Daniel Tufts, the brick-maker and Nathan Tufts of Charlestown. Brothers on this site: William Tufts (1802–1862) and Edward Tufts (1806–1875). Sister Mary married Eliab P. Mackintire, February 20, 1822 (Wyman, p. 969). Cousin and partner: Gilbert Tufts. Wife: Hannah Whitney; marriage published March 12, 1815. Sons on this site: George F. Tufts and Alfred Tufts. Other children named by Wyman, p. 969: James B., Joseph Frothingham, and Henry.

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Deacon Amos Tufts; grandfather Nathan Tufts, cordwainer; uncle Nathan Tufts of Charlestown; uncle Daniel Tufts, brick-maker; brother William Tufts (1802–1862); brother Edward Tufts (1806–1875); son George F. Tufts (1825–1920); son Alfred Tufts (1829–1882); cousin and partner Gilbert Tufts; brother Nathan A. Tufts (1799–1873).

Spouse(s)

  • Hannah (Whitney) Tufts, daughter of Nathaniel and Abigail Whitney of Watertown. Marriage published March 12, 1815; she died August 15, 1872, aged eighty-one years, one month and ten days (Wyman, vol. 2, p. 969). The wedding date itself is not given.
