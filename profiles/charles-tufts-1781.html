Biography

Charles Tufts was a farmer and brick-maker who owned a large part of the land in Charlestown that became Somerville in 1842. He is remembered for giving Walnut Hill, and a large tract of land around it, to the Universalists for the college that bears his name. He was the third son of Daniel Tufts, the Charlestown brick-maker, and a grandson of Nathan Tufts, cordwainer.

“Charles Tufts was a farmer and brick-maker, and a large land-owner in that part of Charlestown set off as Somerville when it was made a separate town. He was a member of the First Universalist Society, and for many years his horse and carriage could be seen every Sunday in one of the sheds which formerly stood in the churchyard of that society, while, with his wife (he had no children), he was occupying his pew in the church.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 164. Internet Archive

“Both Mr. Tufts and his wife took great interest in the Universalist denomination, and he is widely known for his gift of Walnut Hill (now College Hill), and a large tract of land about it, to Tufts College. By this act he became the founder of that institution, which was named in his honor.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 164. Internet Archive

Wyman records him as born July 17, 1781, the son of Daniel Tufts and Abigail Tufts; married Hannah Robinson of Cambridge, daughter of Jacob, who was born April 25, 1795; “donor to the Universalists of the elegant site of Tufts college on Walnut-tree hill, and munificent gifts in money”; died Sunday, December 24, 1876, with no children (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 968. Internet Archive). Sawyer gives his age at death as ninety-five (p. 164). In 1815 and 1817 he bought land of Timothy Walker on the Cambridge road, next to land of his brother Nathan (Wyman, p. 968).

His Land on Walling’s 1855 Map and the Walnut Hill Farm

A 1936 history of Walnut Hill describes Charles Tufts as a farmer and brickmaker like his father, and one of Somerville’s two largest taxpayers in 1842:

“In the fourth generation from Peter Tufts the first, was Daniel Tufts, a farmer and brickmaker, and a large land-owner in the Somerville of today. Charles Tufts, a son of Daniel, was born July 17, 1787 [sic], and likewise became a farmer and brickmaker. His house was on Washington Street, but he owned also extensive farm lands. In 1842 he was one of two persons in the new Somerville who paid a tax amounting to over one hundred dollars. Among the lands taxed to him was that part of Walnut Hill lying in Somerville. His holdings extended also into Medford. Walnut Hill at that time was a hill-top pasture, —a part of the farm of Charles Tufts.”

“Walnut Tree Hill,” Medford Historical Register XXXIX (June 1936), p. 32. View the page scan

The same year the Register printed a reading of H. F. Walling’s map of the Town of Medford (1855), naming the owners of the land beside the Boston & Lowell Railroad. Charles Tufts held nearly the whole western side from Broadway to South Street, apart from the land he had already given to Tufts College:

“The names of the owners of the land adjoining the railroad from Broadway to the Mystic River are shown. On the westerly side, starting at Broadway, we find all the land between the railroad, the Somerville line and South Street, owned by Charles Tufts with the exception of the Medford portion of the tract presented by Mr. Tufts to Tufts College, and a large plot at the South Street end owned by Joseph Manning. The land bounded by the railroad, South Street, the Somerville line and the river is about equally divided among Samuel Tufts, Joseph Manning, Timothy Cotting, J. Q. Adams Estate and J. B. Felt. … Returning to our starting point at Broadway we find the land adjoining the easterly side of the railroad to Harvard Street owned by Charles Tufts and George E. Adams…”

“Medford Railroad Stations,” Medford Historical Register XXXIX (March 1936), p. 4. View the page scan

Tufts association: farm and pasture land owned by Charles Tufts in Somerville and Medford, including Walnut Hill, the site he gave for Tufts College (“Walnut Tree Hill,” Medford Historical Register XXXIX (June 1936), p. 32. View the page scan; “Medford Railroad Stations,” Medford Historical Register XXXIX (March 1936), p. 4. View the page scan).

On the Map: Walling’s Medford, 1855

The town map itself bears out the 1936 reading. In the southern corner of Medford, where the town met Somerville, Walling drew the new Tufts College on the crown of Walnut Hill. The surrounding slopes are lettered with Charles Tufts’s name in three forms, “C. Tufts,” “Chas. Tufts” and “Charles Tufts,” running down to College Station on the Boston & Lowell Railroad and along the Somerville line. Across the tracks the land toward Harvard and Main Streets belongs largely to George E. Adams, while lots marked simply “Tufts” lie near Broadway and Craigie Road. North of the hill, beside the lands of Joseph Manning and Timothy Cotting near South Street, is a small lot marked “S. Tufts” (H. F. Walling, Map of the Town of Medford, Middlesex County, Mass., Surveyed by Order of the Town (Boston, 1855), engraved by A. Kollner. Norman B. Leventhal Map & Education Center, Boston Public Library, call no. G3764.M53 1855 .W3. View the full map).

Service Record

Town: Charlestown and Somerville, Mass. · Dates: 17 July 1781–24 December 1876

Born in 1781, he was too young for Revolutionary service. His father Daniel Tufts appears in the Revolutionary records of Medford; see that page.

Family Connection

Father: Daniel Tufts (1753–1839), brick-maker. Mother: Abigail Tufts (Wyman, p. 968). Brothers: Daniel Tufts Jr. (b. 31 Dec. 1776, tanner, d. 21 Sept. 1868), Gilbert Tufts (b. 27 Apr. 1778, tanner, d. 7 July 1850), and Nathan Tufts “2d” (b. 10 Mar. 1786, brick-maker and farmer of Somerville, d. 1 Aug. 1872), father of the grain dealer Nathan Tufts of Warren Avenue (Wyman, p. 968; Sawyer, pp. 163–164). Uncles: Deacon Amos Tufts and Nathan Tufts of Charlestown. No children.

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Daniel Tufts, brick-maker; grandfather Nathan Tufts, cordwainer; uncle Deacon Amos Tufts; uncle Nathan Tufts of Charlestown; brother Daniel Tufts Jr. (1776–1868); brother Gilbert Tufts (1778–1850); brother Nathan Tufts “2d” (1786–1872).

Spouse(s)

  • Hannah (Robinson) Tufts, of Cambridge, daughter of Jacob Robinson, born April 25, 1795 (Wyman, vol. 2, p. 968). Marriage date not found.
