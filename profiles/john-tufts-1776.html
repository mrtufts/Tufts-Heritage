Biography

John Tufts was born in Medford on July 11, 1776, a son of Jonathan Tufts and Elizabeth (Holden) Tufts (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 161, no. 85-7). He kept a tavern in West Cambridge, the town that is now Arlington, and for nine years owned a wool factory on the brook there. The tavern stood near the old Baptist meeting-house. The Arlington history notes that the old Cutler’s Tavern was “afterward Tufts’s Tavern,” and was still standing in 1880 (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 212. Internet Archive).

In 1804, when West Cambridge built a new meeting-house, the old one was sold at auction in parts: “the tower to John Tufts, for $115” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 110. Internet Archive). The town also chose him clerk of the market (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 121. Internet Archive).

In 1808 he bought the wool-factory of Abner Stearns:

“In 1805 Abner Stearns, of Billerica, bought land here of Ephraim Cooke, victualler, which Stearns, in 1808, sold to John Tufts, with a wool-factory thereon and machinery … Tufts sold these premises to Ezra Trull, of Boston, in 1817, with a mill thereon, and a raceway through land of heirs of Edward Blackington.”

Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 18. Internet Archive (Transcribed from the printed page.)

The works kept his name long after he sold them. A lane “led to the mills formerly known as the ‘Tufts mills,’” which burned about 1831; when Trull sold the site that year the deed described it as the place “where the mills formerly known by the name of the Tufts mills stood, previous to the fire which destroyed said mills” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 18. Internet Archive). See The Tufts Mills, West Cambridge.

His tavern was the centre of the town’s celebrations. The Fourth of July in 1808 was kept “with a procession, military escort, and an oration in Mr. Fiske’s Church, closing with a dinner in an orchard in the rear of Tufts’s tavern” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 121. Internet Archive). In 1811 a great sham fight between volunteer companies was staged near the old Baptist meeting-house, “just above Tufts’s tavern”; the attacking colonel held his infantry “in an orchard in the rear of Tufts’s tavern” and then marched his artillery “through Tufts’s yard and orchards” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 133. Internet Archive).

Service Record

Town: Medford; West Cambridge (now Arlington), Mass. · Dates: 11 July 1776–15 or 16 August 1817

Born in July 1776, he was an infant during the Revolution. No military service is recorded for him in the sources used here.

Family Connection

Father: Jonathan Tufts; mother: Elizabeth (Holden) Tufts. The Arlington register records that “Jonathan Tufts of Medford m. Elizabeth Holden 24 Jan. 1764” (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 311. Internet Archive). The register lists John’s children, baptized in West Cambridge: Rebecca Cutler; Eliza (bap. 1802, m. Abner Peirce 1826); Elmira (bap. 1804); Caroline (bap. 1806, d. 1808); John (bap. 1808, d. 1837), who as “John Tufts, 2d” married Lucy Ann Locke in 1833; Caroline (bap. 1810); and Harris or Harrison (bap. 1812, d. 1827). Tufts Kinsmen also names a son James C. (Vol. I, p. 161).

“John the father (tavern keeper) d. 16 Aug. 1817, a. 41. His wid. m. James Russell of Chas. 13 July, 1833, W. Camb.”

Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 311. Internet Archive

Spouse(s)

  • Rebecca Cutler (1779–1862), daughter of William and Rebecca Cutler; married December 13, 1798 (Benjamin and William R. Cutter, History of the Town of Arlington, Massachusetts (1880), p. 311. Internet Archive; Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 161). As his widow she married James Russell of Charlestown on July 13, 1833.
