Biography

Nathan Tufts, styled “2d” by Wyman, was the youngest son of Daniel Tufts, the Charlestown brick-maker and Abigail Tufts. Wyman records his birth as March 10, 1786, his marriage to Sally Miller on February 21, 1811, and his death on August 1, 1872, aged eighty-six, with the funeral on Monday the 5th (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 968. Internet Archive).

“Nathan Tufts was a brick-maker and farmer living outside the Neck, just beyond the Lowell Railroad bridge which crosses Cambridge Street in Somerville. He was at one time a tanner. He was a correct and thrifty business man, a member of the Harvard Unitarian Society, and was constant in his attendance at that church.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 163. Internet Archive

“He was the father of the late Nathan Tufts, the grain-dealer whose place of business was on Warren Avenue, and who was accidentally killed by being crushed between a vessel and a belay-post on his wharf, October 20, 1887. Mr. Tufts, senior, like all his family, lived to a ripe old age, his death occurring August 1, 1872, when he was eighty-six years old.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 163. Internet Archive

Wyman lists his children: Sarah Elizabeth (b. November 22, 1811, m. Andrew B. Kidder); Mary Tapley (b. October 1, 1813, d. 1833); Martha (b. November 1, 1815); Nathan (b. January 8, 1818, grain-dealer, m. Mary J. Fitz)—the son Sawyer names above; Marcellus (b. April 12, 1820, d. 1822); Hannah Johnson (b. October 28, 1822, m. first Dr. Chauncey Booth, “who has made genealogical investigations,” and second Benjamin F. Allen); Daniel (b. May 6, 1825, d. 1825); and Francis (b. October 13, 1827, Harvard College 1849, LL.B. 1851) (Thomas Bellows Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. 2 (Boston, 1879), p. 968. Internet Archive). Sally Miller was admitted to the Second (Unitarian) Church on November 1, 1835 (p. 968). Wyman also notes purchases of the Rand lot from John Harris’s administrators in 1815, land from S. Ireland’s guardian in 1818, a partition in 1817, and a purchase from John Tapley in 1819 (p. 968).

See also the later firm Nathan Tufts & Sons, grain dealers on Warren Avenue, distinguished on the Tide mill of Nathan Tufts entry from his uncle Nathan Tufts of Charlestown (c. 1764–1835).

Service Record

Town: Charlestown and Somerville, Mass. · Dates: 10 March 1786–1 August 1872

Born in 1786, he was too young for Revolutionary service. No civil office is recorded for him in the sources used here, beyond membership in the Harvard Unitarian Society (Sawyer, p. 163).

Family Connection

Father: Daniel Tufts (1753–1839), brick-maker. Mother: Abigail Tufts (Wyman, p. 968). Brothers: Daniel Tufts Jr. (1776–1868), Gilbert Tufts (1778–1850) and Charles Tufts (1781–1876). Grandfather: Nathan Tufts, cordwainer. Uncles: Deacon Amos Tufts and Nathan Tufts of Charlestown. Wife: Sally Miller, married February 21, 1811. Children as named by Wyman, p. 968, including Nathan (b. 1818), the Warren Avenue grain dealer who died October 20, 1887 (Sawyer, p. 163), and Francis (Harvard 1849).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father Daniel Tufts, brick-maker; brother Daniel Tufts Jr. (1776–1868); brother Gilbert Tufts (1778–1850); brother Charles Tufts (1781–1876); grandfather Nathan Tufts, cordwainer; uncle Deacon Amos Tufts; uncle Nathan Tufts of Charlestown.

Spouse(s)

  • Sally (Miller) Tufts. Married February 21, 1811; admitted to the Second (Unitarian) Church, November 1, 1835 (Wyman, vol. 2, p. 968). Her dates and parentage are not given there.
