Biography

Susannah (also recorded Susan / Susanna) Tufts was the daughter of Cotton Tufts and Mercy Brooks:

“Susanna [dup. Susannah], d. Cotton and Mercy, Jan. 19, 1797. [Susanna, d. Cotton Jr. and Mercy, c.r.i.] [Susannah, G.R.14.]”

Vital Records of Weymouth, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850, vol. I, Births (Boston: NEHGS, 1910). Book scan, births (Transcribed from the printed page.)

“The house now occupied by Major Eliot Pierce was, at the time of which I am writing, the home of the Tufts family, Mr. Quincy Tufts and his sisters Susan and Mercy.”

Edmund Soper Hunt, Weymouth Ways and Weymouth People: Reminiscences (Boston, 1907). Book scan, p. 26 (Transcribed from the printed page.)

With her brother Quincy she gave money, land and buildings that became the Tufts Library:

“The Tufts Library was founded in 1879, when the Town of Weymouth received bequests of money and land from Quincy Tufts and his sister Susannah, grandchildren of Weymouth physician, Dr. Cotton Tufts, for the establishment of a public library.”

Town of Weymouth, “Library History” (Tufts Library founding). Town page

“He and his sister Susannah were the founders and benefactors of the Tufts Library.”

Weymouth Public Libraries, Tufts / Smith / Adams Family Collection finding aid (Collection ID WCT002; prepared C. G. Swank, June 2016). Finding aid (PDF)

She did not marry. After her death and Quincy’s, the town sold remaining Tufts estate lands in Weymouth, Braintree and Quincy (1877 booklet in the family papers).

“Susan” in the County Records

The county records call her Susan. The History of Norfolk County (1918) credits the library to Dr. Cotton Tufts’s grandchildren, “Quincy and Susan Tufts” (Louis A. Cook, ed., History of Norfolk County, Massachusetts, 1622–1918, vol. 1 (1918), p. 384. Internet Archive), and describes its endowment:

“The Tufts Library was founded in 1879, when Quincy Tufts and his sister, Susan Tufts, left a fund of some twenty thousand dollars to be applied by the town “to instructive lectures, the reading room and the general expenses of a free public library.””

Louis A. Cook, ed., History of Norfolk County, Massachusetts, 1622–1918, vol. 1 (1918), p. 418. Internet Archive

Part of the gift was two buildings at the corner of Washington and Commercial Streets, one of which served as the library and reading room until October 1892, when a new building went up on the site (Louis A. Cook, ed., History of Norfolk County, Massachusetts, 1622–1918, vol. 1 (1918), p. 418. Internet Archive). The Norfolk probate index lists her will: “Susan,” Weymouth, 1877, Will, case no. 18,851 (Probate Index, Norfolk County, Massachusetts, 1793–1900, vol. 2 (1910), p. 1090. Internet Archive; see the clipping).

Susan, Susanna and Susannah are forms of the same name, and these records refer to her.

Service Record

Town: Weymouth, Mass. · Dates: 1797–1877

Civilian. Co-founder, by bequest, of the Tufts Library (town library history; finding aid).

Family Connection

Father: Cotton Tufts (1757–1833). Mother: Mercy Brooks Tufts. Brother: Quincy Tufts. Sisters Lucy (Tarbell) and Mercy. Grandfather: Dr. Cotton Tufts. Unmarried; no children.

Relatives with profiles on this site: father; mother; brother Quincy; grandfather Dr. Cotton Tufts.

Spouse(s)

Unmarried (town library history; Weymouth finding aid).
