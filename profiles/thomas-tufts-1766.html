Biography

Thomas Tufts of Lexington was one of the twelve children of Peter Tufts of Winter Hill and Anne Adams Tufts, raised on the family farm on Winter Hill. The Adams family genealogy of 1898 records:

“Thomas Tufts, b. May 18, 1766; m. Nov. 29, 1791, Rebecca Adams, dau. of John and Lucy (Hubbard) Adams of Lincoln, Mass., d, 20 Feb., 1858. He d. 10 June, 1830; res. Lexington, Mass.”

Andrew N. Adams, A Genealogical History of Henry Adams of Braintree, Mass., and His Descendants; also John Adams of Cambridge, Mass. (1898), p. 973. Book scan (Internet Archive) (Transcribed from the printed page.)

“Rebecca, b. in Lincoln, Feb. 28. 1767; m. Nov. 19, 1791, Thomas Tufts, a large farmer of Lexington, Mass., son of Peter, Jr., and Anna (Adams) Tufts, b. May 18, 1766, d. 10 June, 1830. She d. 20 Feb., 1858, age 91 years.”

Andrew N. Adams, A Genealogical History of Henry Adams of Braintree, Mass., and His Descendants; also John Adams of Cambridge, Mass. (1898), p. 978. Book scan (Internet Archive) (Transcribed from the printed page.)

The same genealogy lists their children: Thomas, born 1792, “drowned in fording a river in Kentucky, 1816, age 24”; Rebecca, who died at 30; Marshall, Harvard 1827, died 1855; Eveline, who married a Rochester of Ohio; and Lucy Ann, who married Dr. Proctor of Castine, Maine (Adams, p. 1004).

Selectman of Lexington

Hudson’s History of Lexington records that Thomas Tufts served the town as selectman in 1799 and 1800:

“Thomas Tufts, b. May 18, 1766, m. Nov. 29, 1791, Rebecca Adams of Lincoln, b. Feb. 28, 1767. He d. June 10, 1830, aged 64. She d. Feb. 20, 1858, aged 91. He was selectman, 1799 and 1800.”

Charles Hudson, History of the Town of Lexington, Middlesex County, Massachusetts (1868), p. 247. Internet Archive; the selectmen list on p. 404 gives “Thomas Tufts, 1799, 1800.”

The family place gave its name to a landmark on the road to Lincoln: Weston Street ran “to Lincoln line, passing near the Tufts Place” (Charles Hudson, History of the Town of Lexington, Middlesex County, Massachusetts (1868), p. 444. Internet Archive).

Hudson adds that their son Marshall, born September 26, 1802, graduated from Harvard College in 1827 and studied theology with Dr. Holmes of Cambridge. “He wrote and published several small volumes, one of which was, ‘The Shores of Vespucci,’ a romance” (Charles Hudson, History of the Town of Lexington, Middlesex County, Massachusetts (1868), p. 247. Internet Archive).

Son Thomas’s death: Hudson records that the son Thomas, born December 16, 1792, “was drowned in Ky., Oct. 8, 1817” (Charles Hudson, History of the Town of Lexington, Middlesex County, Massachusetts (1868), p. 247. Internet Archive), and the family record agrees: he drowned fording the Licking River, Kentucky, on October 8, 1817, after serving as a private in the War of 1812 (Tufts Kinsmen, Vol. I, p. 243, no. 133-1). The year 1817 is followed on this site; the Adams genealogy quoted above gives 1816.
