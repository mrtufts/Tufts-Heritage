Biography

Quincy Tufts, son of Cotton Tufts and Mercy Brooks, was born in Weymouth on Independence Day, 1791:

“Quincy, s. Cotton Jr. [dup. omits Jr.] [Jr., c.r.i.] and Mercy, July 4, 1791.”

Vital Records of Weymouth, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850, vol. I, Births (Boston: NEHGS, 1910). Book scan, births (Transcribed from the printed page.)

Boston merchant

“Mr. Tufts, a bachelor, kept a store on Washington street in Boston, for many years. This was located nearly opposite the Old South Church and was one of the best known stores of Boston, where he carried such a variety of dry goods that one could match his buttons, braids, silks, bandana handkerchiefs, etc. Mr. Tufts saved all his wrapping paper, assorted it, and whenever a customer bought an article he carefully selected a piece of just the size for wrapping. He never wasted anything but time. At the close of life he left his fortune, — some twenty thousand dollars, — to found the Tufts Library in his native town.”

Edmund Soper Hunt, Weymouth Ways and Weymouth People: Reminiscences (Boston, 1907). Book scan, pp. 26–27 (Transcribed from the printed page.)

“Quincy Tufts kept a store on Washington St. in Boston which carried dry goods. He left his fortune ($20k) to Tufts Library. He and his sister Susannah were the founders and benefactors of the Tufts Library.”

Weymouth Public Libraries, Tufts / Smith / Adams Family Collection finding aid (Collection ID WCT002; prepared C. G. Swank, June 2016). Finding aid (PDF) (Transcribed from the printed page.)

Tufts Library

“The Tufts Library was founded in 1879, when the Town of Weymouth received bequests of money and land from Quincy Tufts and his sister Susannah, grandchildren of Weymouth physician, Dr. Cotton Tufts, for the establishment of a public library. Part of the bequest included two wooden buildings at the corner of Washington and Commercial Streets in Weymouth Landing.”

Town of Weymouth, “Library History” (Tufts Library founding). Town page

He never married. See Places: Tufts Library.

The Library in the County History

The History of Norfolk County (1918) names Quincy Tufts and his sister Susan as founders of the Tufts Library in 1879, leaving “a fund of some twenty thousand dollars” and two buildings at Washington and Commercial Streets (Louis A. Cook, ed., History of Norfolk County, Massachusetts, 1622–1918, vol. 1 (1918), pp. 384, 418. Internet Archive). See Susannah Tufts.

Invited to Dinner, 1808

Abigail Adams’s invitation of 31 December 1807, asking “Mr and Mrs Tufts” of Weymouth to dinner on New Year’s Day “together with Mr Quincy Tufts and Miss Lucy,” names this Quincy, then sixteen, and his sister Lucy. The note was sold at RR Auction in 2014 and again in 2025; the texts and links are on the page of his father, Cotton Tufts (1757–1833).

Family Connection

Father: Cotton Tufts (1757–1833). Mother: Mercy Brooks Tufts. Sister and co-founder: Susannah Tufts (1797–1877). Sisters Lucy (Tarbell) and Mercy. Grandfather: Dr. Cotton Tufts. Unmarried; no children (Hunt; finding aid).

Relatives with profiles on this site: father; mother; sister Susannah; grandfather Dr. Cotton Tufts.
