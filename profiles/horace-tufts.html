Biography

Horace Tufts was a shipmaster in the Salem and Beverly customs district in the mid-nineteenth century. On 8 April 1835 he arrived at New York from St. Jago (Santiago), Cape Verde, on the brig Gambia, listed as a mariner, age 38, one of several “distressed seamen” put on board by the American consul (National Archives microfilm M237, reel 26, list 144; transcript of the manifest). That age points to a birth about 1796 or 1797.

The printed Salem and Beverly ship registers name him as master of the brig Ruby, 126 tons, built at Sedgwick, Maine, in 1839, registered 5 August 1847, with owners Edward D. Kimball and John Bertram (A. Frank Hitchings and Stephen Willard Phillips, Ship Registers of the District of Salem and Beverly, 1789–1900 (Salem: Essex Institute, 1906), p. 161. Internet Archive). They also name him as master of the bark Lady Suffolk, 199 tons, registered 17 October 1860, William Calley owner (A. Frank Hitchings and Stephen Willard Phillips, Ship Registers of the District of Salem and Beverly, 1789–1900 (Salem: Essex Institute, 1906), p. 103. Internet Archive).

Letter from the Brig Tam O’Shanter, 1848

A bookseller, R & A Petrilla of Roosevelt, New Jersey, has listed a 1½-page autograph letter signed by Horace Tufts, written at Matacong (Matakong, Guinea) on 15 December 1848 and addressed to Edward D. Kimball, the Salem merchant who appears as an owner of both the Ruby and the brig Tam O’Shanter. The listing quotes him as writing, in part, that he is “very sorry to inform you that the Tam O Shanter is in such a very bad condition that I am afraid she will not be fit to come home unless there is a great deal of money laid out,” that he had “been up the Coast in her and disposed of part of her Cargo and the Remnant of Ruby Powder,” and that he had left his sick second mate at Sierra Leone to come home in the brig Ohio, his chief mate having also been sick. He asked Kimball to pay the second mate on his return and promised to “do all in my power to forward the voyage.” The listing is at AbeBooks (asked $252 at the time it was checked).

The same registers describe the brig Tam O’Shanter, 123 tons, built at Essex in 1833, as registered 18 March 1848 with Edward D. Kimball as owner and Collins Ingalls as master (A. Frank Hitchings and Stephen Willard Phillips, Ship Registers of the District of Salem and Beverly, 1789–1900 (Salem: Essex Institute, 1906), p. 181. Internet Archive). Horace Tufts is not named as master on that register entry; the December 1848 letter is the evidence that he was then in command of the vessel off the West African coast.

No birth, marriage, or death line for this Horace Tufts was confirmed in the Salem vital records searched in this pass, and he has not been attached to a Medford or Charlestown family already on this site. The age 38 on the 1835 manifest is the only evidence used here for a birth about 1796–97. The Tam O’Shanter. The March 1848 Salem register names Collins Ingalls, not Horace Tufts, as master; the letter of 15 December 1848 is known from the bookseller’s extract, not from the original sheet. The bookseller’s further claims that he was employed in the triangle trade, that he “plied some of the most notorious slave-trading ports,” and that he later sued the English government over an accusation of slave trading, are the seller’s commentary and are not in the quoted letter.
