Biography

Nathan Tufts of Charlestown, the butcher, tanner and mill owner, was the son of Nathan Tufts, a cordwainer who farmed chiefly in Medford, and the brother of Daniel Tufts and Deacon Amos Tufts. Sawyer traces that elder Nathan as a son of Peter Tufts of Milk Row.

“One of the sons of this Peter was Nathan Tufts, by trade a cordwainer, but chiefly occupied as a farmer in Medford. He was the father of Daniel, Deacon Amos, and Nathan Tufts, who for the greater part of their lives were prominent citizens of the old town and among its leading business men.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 162. Internet Archive

The younger Nathan, Sawyer wrote, “was a noted man in the old town.” He began as a butcher and packed beef on a large scale, then became widely known for the quality of the sole-leather he tanned.

“He was originally a butcher, and did a large business in packing beef. He was also a tanner, widely known by the superior quality of the sole-leather manufactured by him, the popularity of which, for many years, gave a good reputation to the town as well as to himself. Mr. Tufts had also a wharf, where he dealt in wood and lumber; and a grist-mill made a part of his establishment.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 167. Internet Archive

He built the mill pond at Charlestown Neck and ran a tide mill there, later remembered as “a tide-mill just below Sullivan square.” A lantern slide in the Boston Public Library’s Charlestown collection, titled “Tide mill of Nathan Tufts,” shows the shingled mill buildings; the nearer gable bears the painted sign “NATHAN TUFTS & SON.” The full account of the mill, the 1848 plan of Tufts Mill Pond, and the later history of the site is on Tide mill of Nathan Tufts. See also Places: Homesteads and the Mystic River.

“His tannery and wharf were on the Mystic-river side of the town, and a good many of the town's people found steady employment there. Besides this, he built the Mill-pond at the Neck, and was connected with Abel Fitz in the grain and meal business there. He also started a new tannery there, and carried it on as long as he lived. It was afterwards sold to Chester Guild & Sons”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 167–168. Internet Archive

Sawyer also places that pond “by the side of Maiden Bridge,” with the mills grinding (p. 463). He lived in the house on Chelsea Street first built and occupied by Aaron Putnam, later known as the home of Dr. Lambert (pp. 168, 171).

“Nathan Tufts died in October, 1835, aged seventy-one years.”

Timothy T. Sawyer, Old Charlestown: Historical, Biographical, Reminiscent (Boston, 1902), p. 168. Internet Archive

He had no children. By his will, after provision for his wife and a few bequests, among them $1,000 to the Bunker Hill Monument Association, the bulk of his property went to his nephews and nieces, the children of Daniel and Deacon Amos (p. 168). His widow Mary later testified in a suit over the estate (p. 169). Among the heirs on this site are the sons and grandsons of Deacon Amos who took charge of the Medford land, including the Dexter farm.

This is not Nathan Tufts (1741–1784) of Medford, Nathan Tuffs (1752–1827) of Portsmouth and Bethel, or Nathan Tufts Jr. of Medford, son of William Tufts 4th.

Service Record

Town: Charlestown, Mass. · Dates: c. 1764–October 1835

No military service has been matched. His public and business offices, as Sawyer records them (p. 168), were civil: he was one of the first directors of the Bunker Hill Bank, an early advocate of free bridges and an incorporator of Warren Bridge, and one of the founders of the Harvard Unitarian Society.

Family Connection

Father: Nathan Tufts, a cordwainer and Medford farmer, son of Peter Tufts of Milk Row (Sawyer, p. 162). The Adams genealogy (p. 974) names the mother of his sons Amos and this Nathan as Mary Adams. Brothers: Daniel Tufts (a brick-maker and farmer living outside the Neck; legislature, 1811–12) and Deacon Amos Tufts (1762–1839). Neither brother has a confirmed profile on this site. He had no children. His nephews and nieces, the children of Daniel and Amos, were his principal heirs (Sawyer, p. 168).

Relatives with profiles on this site: nephew William Tufts (1802–1862), son of Deacon Amos; nephew Edward Tufts (1806–1875), son of Deacon Amos; great-nephew George F. Tufts (1825–1920); great-nephew Alfred Tufts (1829–1882).

Spouse(s)

  • Mary (maiden name not given). She survived him and, as “Mary Tufts, the widow of Nathan,” testified in the 1837 suit over his estate (Sawyer, Old Charlestown, p. 169).

Business

Nathan Tufts (c. 1764–1835), the Charlestown butcher and tanner who bequeathed land to the sons of his brothers Daniel and Amos, built the mill pond at Charlestown Neck and ran a tide mill, tannery and wharf there. See Tide mill of Nathan Tufts.
