Biography

The name Elias Tuffts appears on the published Lexington-alarm roll of Capt. Samuel Barnard’s Watertown company. Frank Warren Coburn, The Battle of April 19, 1775 (Lexington, 1912), p. 47–48, prints the company heading on p. 47 (“WATERTOWN. Captain Samuel Barnard’s Company. Entered the Contest at Arlington,” from “Lexington Alarms, XI, 214”) and continues the private’s names on p. 48, where “Elias Tuffts” stands among Watertown and Cambridge-area names (Coolidge, Bond, Learnard, Whitney, and others).

The same service is in Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (Boston, 1908), p. 130: “TuFFTS, Elias. Private, Capt. Samuel Barnard’s co. of militia, Col. Thomas Gardner’s regt., which marched on the alarm of April 19, 1775; service, 2 days. Roll endorsed ‘Watertown.’”

Identity caveat: this roll entry may be the same person as Elias Tufts (b. 1751), born 13 July 1751 at Charlestown, who was of Watertown in 1774 when he married Sarah Fillebrown, and who later lived at Pembroke, New Hampshire. That existing profile had not been checked against the Soldiers and Sailors Tuffs spelling, and reports no service found in the Tufts entries of vol. XVI. The spelling, the Watertown endorsement, and the 1774 residence make a single identification likely, but it is not proved here. This page records the roll name and the two printed sources only; it does not invent a second man’s biography. He is not Elias Tufts, wheelwright (1787–1868), the later Medford wheelwright.
