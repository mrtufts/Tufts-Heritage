This page follows the Shea family of Annascaul, on the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry, and the Selee, Ziegler, and Layer families of Sebewaing, Michigan. Every confirmed statement is cited. Anything that still rests only on family tradition, or that the records contradict or leave unsettled, is marked Unverified.

The page is kept off the public menus and is not offered to search engines.

Shea of Annascaul

Family research names James Shea and Joanne (also given as Johanna) Crean as Shannon’s great-great-grandparents. They are said to have lived and died in Annascaul, a village on the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry. Kerry Ancestors, asked to look for an earlier generation, reportedly found no records older than this couple. Shannon holds a copy of their marriage certificate.

Irish civil registration of births, marriages, and deaths, searchable at irishgenealogy.ie, begins in 1864. Catholic registers for the parish of Ballanvohir (Annascaul) are among those filmed by the National Library of Ireland. Those collections were not successfully searched from this desk during the present check, so the certificate itself has not been compared here with the civil or parish entry.

⚑ Unverified

That James Shea and Joanne (Johanna) Crean lived and died in Annascaul; the date, place, and wording of their marriage; and that no earlier Shea generation can be traced in Kerry. Family research, including a report of a query to Kerry Ancestors and a marriage certificate not transcribed here. Record not yet located in a freely available index.

Michael Shea and Black Brook

Family research says that James and Joanne’s only son, Michael, Shannon’s great-grandfather, emigrated to the United States and settled in Black Brook, Clinton County, New York.

⚑ Unverified

Michael Shea’s parentage, baptism, emigration, settlement in Black Brook, and the claim that he was the couple’s only son. Family research; record not yet located. A census household, naturalization, or death record naming his parents would be the first place to look.

James Shea and Shea’s Market, Lake Placid

Family research says that one of Michael’s sons, James Shea, Shannon’s grandfather, moved to Lake Placid and opened Shea’s Market.

A James Shea of Lake Placid is well attested in public records, though they do not, in the sources seen, name his father. He is buried at Saint Agnes Cemetery in Lake Placid, with years 1866–1941 on the memorial. His wife, Grace A. Obrey Shea (1885–1951), is buried in the same cemetery. The memorials name their children as James Obrey Shea (1909–1972), John Amos “Jack” Shea (1910–2002), Eugene William Patrick Shea (1912–2017), and Grace Amelia Shea (1917–2000).

Find a Grave memorials: James Shea (1866–1941), memorial 42402083; Grace A. Obrey Shea (1885–1951), memorial 42402003; both Saint Agnes Cemetery, Lake Placid, Essex County, New York. Accessed 10 November 2026.

James Shea represented Essex County in the New York State Assembly for four one-year terms, 1908 through 1911. He was the Republican nominee at the election of 2 November 1909.

The Political Graveyard, “New York: State Assembly, 1900s” and “1910s”: “Essex County: … James Shea 1908–11.” Election entry, Essex County, 2 November 1909: “James Shea (Rep), elected.” 1900s list · 1910s list. Also H. Res. 340, 107th Cong. (agreed to 6 February 2002), which names Jack Shea “the son of James Shea, a New York State Assemblyman, and Grace Shea.” govinfo.gov.

A published obituary of his son Eugene states that Eugene was born 9 March 1912 in Lake Placid to James and Grace (Obrey) Shea, that his father owned Shea’s Market and was a four-term representative in the New York Legislature, and that Eugene’s siblings were James Jr., Jack, Grace, and Edmund Shea, all then deceased. It also records a former marriage to Ina M. Selee.

Obituary of Eugene William Patrick “Gene” Shea, Tampa Bay Times, 7 October 2017, as reproduced on Find a Grave memorial 201321582 (Eugene William Patrick Shea, 9 March 1912, Lake Placid – 3 October 2017, Saint Petersburg, Florida; burial, Saint Agnes Cemetery, Lake Placid). Accessed 10 November 2026.

James Shea of Lake Placid, later inactive by dissolution or annulment of authority on 24 March 1993. The extract gives the county of registration as Erie, which does not match Lake Placid (Essex County) and has not been checked against the official Department of State entry.

The Crean connection and Tom Crean

Family research says that Joanne Crean was a great-aunt of the Antarctic explorer Tom Crean, who was born and raised on a farm in Annascaul, served on Ernest Shackleton’s Endurance expedition, and later opened the South Pole Inn in the village. The family visited the inn about three years before this page was written (about 2023). It was then under later ownership and still displayed Tom Crean’s history.

The explorer’s own life is well documented. Thomas “Tom” Crean was born at Gurtuchrane (also written Gortacurrane or Gortacurraun), near Annascaul, County Kerry, in 1877, and died in 1938. He enlisted in the Royal Navy as a youth and served on Robert Falcon Scott’s British National Antarctic Expedition of 1901–04 (Discovery) and British Antarctic Expedition of 1910–13 (Terra Nova), and on Shackleton’s Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition of 1914–16 (Endurance). On the return from the Terra Nova polar journey he walked some 35 miles alone to Hut Point to bring help for the desperately ill Lieutenant Edward Evans; he and William Lashly received the Albert Medal. On Endurance he was second officer, took part in the boat journey to Elephant Island, and crossed South Georgia with Shackleton and Frank Worsley. He was invalided out of the navy in March 1920 and settled in Annascaul. He is buried at Ballynacourty, near his birthplace.

Scott Polar Research Institute, Cambridge, “Crean, Thomas” (dates 1877–1938), in Meet the pioneers. spri.cam.ac.uk. Accessed 10 November 2026.

His parents were Patrick Crean and Catherine (Kate) Courtney or Cournane of Gortacurrane, who married in Annascaul in 1867. A compiled account based on civil and parish entries at irishgenealogy.ie lists their children, among them Hugh (1867), Mary (1869), John (1870), Cornelius (1871), Daniel (1873), Patrick (1875), Thomas (1877), Johanna, Michael, and Catherine. The same parish also had earlier Creans, including a Johanna Crean baptized in 1855 who later married Maurice Curran, of the generation of Tom’s father.

Kay Caball, “Tracing Tom Crean’s Ancestors,” My Kerry Ancestors, citing IrishGenealogy.ie civil records and the Annascaul (Ballanvohir) parish registers. mykerryancestors.com. Accessed 10 November 2026. See also the compiled guide at tomcreanbook.com.

Published sources disagree on when the South Pole Inn received that name. The Scott Polar Research Institute says that Crean opened a pub of that name in 1927. A later account, citing an indenture of 8 December 1916 and later testimony, says he purchased a licensed dwelling house in Annascaul in December 1916 and rebuilt and named it the South Pole Inn in 1929.

SPRI biography, as above, “in 1927 he opened a pub named the South Pole Inn.” Tim Foley, “The South Pole Inn: A Complete History,” citing an indenture dated 8 December 1916 between Catherine Curran and Thomas Crean and describing a 1929 rebuilding. tomcreanbook.com. Accessed 10 November 2026.

⚑ Unverified

That Joanne (Johanna) Crean, wife of James Shea, was a great-aunt of Tom Crean. Family research; a documentary link has not been found in the sources seen. Several women named Johanna or Joanna Crean lived in the same parish, including a sister and an aunt of the explorer. The marriage certificate Shannon holds, especially any statement of the bride’s father, would be the record most likely to prove or disprove the relationship. Also unverified: the exact year the family visited the inn, and whether the house was first named the South Pole Inn in 1927 or 1929.

The Selee line

The Selee line, as set out on family charts, comes down to Ina Mae Selee, who married Eugene W. D. Shea of Lake Placid. A note on the charts says “Selee is French.”

Social Security records identify Ina M. Shea, also recorded as Ina May Shea and Ina Maye Selee, as the daughter of Norman S. Selee and Gladys K. Smith. They give her birth as 6 December 1917 (the charts say 4 December 1917) and her death as 17 December 2005, with a last residence in ZIP Code 33716, in the Saint Petersburg, Florida, area. The same files give the birthplace variously as Detroit or, in one entry, the United Kingdom; the charts give Flint, Genesee County, Michigan. A sister, Lina Marie Selee, is recorded with the same parents and the same birth date of 6 December 1917 in Detroit. Another sister, Shirley Anne Selee, was born in 1920 or 1921.

Shea / Ina May Shea / Ina Maye Selee, SSN 261-18-0528, born 6 December 1917, died 17 December 2005, parents Norman S. Selee and Gladys K. Smith; also Lina Marie Selee (6 December 1917 – 19 July 2000) and Shirley Anne Selee (born 14 November 1920 or 1921, died 19 March 2006). sortedbyname.com. Accessed 10 November 2026.

Eugene William Patrick Shea’s published obituary names a former marriage to Ina M. Selee, deceased. The charts give his name as Eugene W. D. Shea and place the marriage at Lake Placid.

Obituary of Eugene William Patrick Shea, Tampa Bay Times, 7 October 2017, on Find a Grave memorial 201321582, as above.

The charts name Ina’s father as Norman Sylvester Selee, born 16 October 1893 at Sebewaing, Michigan, and divorced 20 April 1919; and her mother as Gladys Knight Smith Selee, born 29 April 1894 at Saginaw, Michigan, died 2 June 1971 at Saint Petersburg. They name Norman’s parents as William P. Selee, born 30 May 1855 in New York, married 1883, died 11 December 1920, said to have owned a cold-storage business; and Mary Anne Ziegler, called his second wife, born 11 December 1863 at Sebewaing, died 7 May 1918 there, and buried at Memorial Cemetery.

Cemetery memorials identify the husband of Mary Anna Ziegler as Peter William Selee, born 30 May 1855 at Bay City, Bay County, Michigan, died 11 December 1929 at Daytona Beach, Florida, and buried at Memorial Cemetery, Sebewaing. The memorials give the marriage year as 1883. A later wife, Regina Margarethe Kundinger Selee (1863–1945), is listed with a marriage in 1921. Mary Anna Ziegler Selee’s own memorial matches the charts on her birth (11 December 1863, Sebewaing), death (7 May 1918, Sebewaing), and burial at Memorial Cemetery. A Social Security application for Grant Horace Selee, born 18 April 1895 at Sebewaing, names his parents as Peter Selee and Mary Zeigler, which fits Peter William and Mary Anna, and does not fit a different man.

Find a Grave: Peter William Selee (30 May 1855, Bay City, Michigan – 11 December 1929, Daytona Beach, Florida), memorial 36479601, Memorial Cemetery, Sebewaing; Mary Anna Ziegler Selee (11 December 1863 – 7 May 1918), memorial 36479602, same cemetery, parents given as Johann Frederich Ziegler and Catherine Sophia Layer Ziegler, spouse Peter William Selee, married 1883. Grant Horace Selee, Social Security Form SS-5 dated December 1936, as compiled at SortedByName: father Peter Selee, mother Mary Zeigler, born 18 April 1895, Sebewaing. Accessed 10 November 2026.

The family charts list Grace A. Selee (1885), Mabel Selee (m. Fred, 1886), Norman S. Selee (1893), and Grant H. Selee (1895). One heading on the charts labels them “Children of Norman/Gladys,” but the dates belong to William and Mary Anne’s generation, not Ina’s. Find a Grave, as of this check, names only Mabel I. Selee Neigenfind (1886–1954) among the children of Peter William and Mary Anna; that list is incomplete relative to the Social Security application for Grant.

The Ziegler and Layer families of Sebewaing

Sebewaing Township, in Huron County on Saginaw Bay, was organized in 1853. A compiled township history names Frederick Ziegler as a constable at the first town meeting and as supervisor in 1854, and John F. Ziegler as supervisor from 1875 to 1882. Those offices fit the years of the immigrant Frederick and of John Frederick, but the printed list does not identify the officeholders by birth or parentage.

“History of Sebewaing Township,” Genealogy Trails, Huron County, Michigan (compiled from local historical sources): first election, constables include Frederick Ziegler; supervisors, “Frederick Ziegler 1854” and “John F. Ziegler 1875–82.” genealogytrails.com. Accessed 10 November 2026.

Cemetery memorials for the family of Mary Anna Ziegler Selee give her father as Johann Frederich Ziegler, born 3 March 1834 in Germany, died 4 February 1909 at Sebewaing, buried at New Salem Lutheran Church Cemetery there; and her mother as Catherine Sophia Layer Ziegler, born 17 March 1840, died 5 September 1878, buried in the same cemetery. Those death dates, and Johann Frederich’s birth date, match the family charts. The charts give Catherine’s birth as 28 March 1840, eleven days later than the memorial, and add Steinbach, Württemberg, as her birthplace. The memorials name a later wife of Johann Frederich, Sophia Grettenberger Ziegler (1835–1906), married 1879, after Catherine’s death. Catherine’s children on the memorial include Joseph Julius (1859–1945) through John D. (1876–1958), with Mary Anna among them, which is consistent with a marriage in the late 1850s. The charts give that marriage as 23 February 1859.

Find a Grave: Johann Frederich Ziegler (3 March 1834, Germany – 4 February 1909, Sebewaing), memorial 36142284, New Salem Lutheran Church Cemetery, Sebewaing; Catherine Sophia Layer Ziegler (17 March 1840 – 5 September 1878), memorial 36142278, same cemetery; Mary Anna Ziegler Selee, memorial 36479602, as above. Accessed 10 November 2026.

The charts take Johann Frederich’s parents back to Frederick Ziegler, born 6 March 1803 at Osternohe, Middle Franconia, Bavaria, married 27 June 1830, died 14 September 1878 at Sebewaing, and Kunigunda Hutzler (born 1803), with a second marriage to Anna Christina Krauss (born 22 December 1813 at Wieseth). They name Frederick’s parents as Kasper Ziegler and Kunigunda Krauser, and Kunigunda Hutzler’s father as Andreas Hutzler, and they note an 1840 immigration from a place read as Utzmannsbach, Middle Franconia. Johann Frederich’s Find a Grave memorial, however, names his father as Johann Ziegler (1815–1893), which does not match that earlier couple. The two accounts have not been reconciled.

For the Layer side, the charts name Catherine’s parents as Johannes Jacob Layer, born 1801, died 1860, and Anna Marie Layer, née Epple, born 1807, and they say Catherine immigrated on 25 June 1846 in the ship Prince Albert. A compiled passenger list for the Prince Albert, from London, arriving at New York on 25 June 1846, does include a Layer family from Württemberg in steerage. Among them are Johann Layer, age 45 (birth year about 1801, consistent with Johannes Jacob); Catharina Layer, age 5; Ann Maria Layer, age 29; and Margaretta Layer, age 38, together with Gottleb, age 39, and several children. The presence of both an Ann Maria, aged 29, and a Margaretta, aged 38, means the list does not by itself identify Anna Marie (née Epple, born 1807 on the charts, who would have been about 39 in 1846) as Johann’s wife. Catharina, listed as age 5, is a little younger than a child born in March 1840 would have been in June 1846. The list is consistent with the family’s account, but it does not by itself prove that this Catharina is the Catherine Sophia Layer who later died at Sebewaing.

“Passengers on the Prince Albert departing from London, arriving 25 June 1846 in New York,” compiled from the arrival manifest, including LAYER, JOHANN, age 45; LAYER, CATHARINA, age 5; LAYER, ANN MARIA, age 29; LAYER, MARGARETTA, age 38, all from Württemberg, steerage. shipmanifests.com. Accessed 10 November 2026. The original manifest images are in the New York passenger lists (National Archives), also indexed on FamilySearch and Ancestry.

The charts also write a marriage date of 1859 against Johannes Jacob Layer, who is there said to have died in 1860, which cannot be right if the family sailed in 1846; that heading appears to have been mixed with John Frederick’s 1859 marriage.
