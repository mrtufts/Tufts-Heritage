Charles Tufts and Walnut Hill

Charles Tufts of Somerville gave the hilltop land on which Tufts College was built.

“As early as 1841, Charles Tufts conceived the idea that an Universalist College was needed, and that his Walnut Hill was the appropiate site for it. … when previously asked by a friend what he intended to do with the wind-swept height, Mr. Tufts replied; “I will put a light on it”, —a remark which has become one of the traditions of Tufts University. … A charter for the new College was issued April 15, 1852, and on July 23, 1853 the corner stone for Ballou Hall, the first building, was laid; and in 1854 was opened to students.”

Jay Franklin Tufts, Tufts Family History 1633–1963 (secondary source), p. 139 · page scan

“Mr. Charles Tufts, whose name is better known to the people than almost any other, whose generous acts endeared him to many he had benefacted, is no more. … He believed in doing good to the greatest number, and hence his noble gift to Tufts College, of the beautiful tract of land upon which the buildings are now located.”

Jay Franklin Tufts, Tufts Family History 1633–1963 (secondary source), p. 143, quoting the Somerville Journal, December 30, 1876 · page scan

The Weymouth Line and Tufts Library

“The year 1879 was a memorable one for the town of Weymouth. Early that year, through the generosity of Quincy and his sister Miss Susanna Tufts, they gave to the town, by will, a fund for the establishment of a library for the benefit of its citizens.”

Jay Franklin Tufts, Tufts Family History 1633–1963 (secondary source), p. 35 · page scan
