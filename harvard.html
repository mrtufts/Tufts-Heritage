Harvard College in Cambridge educated three generations of Medford Tufts physicians and a string of ministers and schoolmasters. This page gathers every Tufts tie to Harvard found so far in the printed records: degrees, offices and honors. Class years come from Sibley’s Harvard Graduates (through the class of 1771) and the Quinquennial Catalogue of 1915.

Tufts association: Tufts graduates of Harvard College from 1701, among them Medford’s first physician, Dr. Simon Tufts (class of 1724), his son Dr. Simon Tufts (1744) and Dr. Cotton Tufts (1749), who received an honorary M.D. in 1785. (Brooks, pp. 302–306; Quinquennial Catalogue (1915), classes of 1724, 1744, 1749)

Three Doctors, Two Simons

“He was born Jan. 16, 1727, and graduated at Harvard College in 1744.”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 305. Text (Perseus)

“Dr. Cotton Tufts, born 1732, brother of the above, graduated at Harvard College 1749; studied medicine with his brother; settled in Weymouth”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 306. Text (Perseus)

The elder Simon trained his eldest son. The son, in turn, trained his younger brother Cotton and, later, John Brooks, the future governor (Brooks, pp. 305–307). The Countway Library’s description of Cotton’s papers gives his degrees as “A.B., 1749, honorary M.D., 1785, Harvard College.” ArchiveGrid record.

⚑ Unverified

Cotton Tufts’s birth year. Brooks (p. 306) and the Adams Papers editors give 1732; Historic Leaves, vol. 3, p. 18, gives May 3, 1734.

Offices, Honors and Gifts

  • Honorary M.D., 1785: Dr. Cotton Tufts (Quinquennial Catalogue, class of 1749).
  • Harvard and the Medical Society: Brooks writes that among the members of the Massachusetts Medical Society “Dr. Tufts’s name stands the second, in the order of time, on the college catalogue” (p. 306), meaning the younger Dr. Simon.
  • Gifts: No gift or bequest to Harvard by a colonial Tufts has yet been found.
  • Not Harvard: No Harvard degree has been found for Rev. Francis Tufts (1744–1833), the Freewill Baptist minister, in the Sibley index or the 1915 catalogue. The only Francis Tufts there is an LL.B. of 1851. Tufts College in Medford (1852) is a separate institution.

⚑ Unverified

Harvard University Archives records (faculty records, quarter-bill and steward’s books) have not been searched. Offices held at Harvard by any Tufts are unconfirmed.
