The Story

Research in progress

Albert C. Tufts. He was a druggist in Sacramento, California, in the eighteen seventies through the eighteen nineties. City directories track him year by year: clerk with Justin Gates and Brother in eighteen seventy, apothecary with J. L. Polhemus in eighteen seventy-four, back with Justin Gates in eighteen seventy-five, then druggist at the southeast corner of Tenth and J Streets by eighteen seventy-nine. He stayed at that corner for years. He was proprietor of Meadows’ Magic Liniment and Tufts’ Yerbine Cough Balsam, and in eighteen seventy-seven he and a partner registered a California trademark, number four oh four, for Yerba Santa Cough Balsam. He married Eva N. Carriger around December eighteen seventy-seven, per the Pacific Bee newspaper. Their children included Edith, born October seventh, eighteen seventy-eight, and Jessie May. The family worshipped at St. Paul’s Episcopal Church in Sacramento. Edith later taught at Union Primary School. Albert disappears from the directories in eighteen ninety-nine, suggesting he died around eighteen ninety-eight or ninety-nine. The site flags this as research in progress, so the death date is inference, not a confirmed record.

Sources

J and Tenth, res. 1605 [H]; proprietor of Meadows’ Magic Liniment and Tufts’ Yerbine Cough Balsam); 1888–89 (druggist, 1000 J); 1894 (druggist, 1000 J, r. 1605 H); 1896 (druggist, 1000 J, telephone 379); 1897–99 and 1902 (family entries); 1899 (Albert not listed). Quoted as printed; printed page numbers not yet captured.
  • The Pacific Bee, Sacramento, Dec. 22, 1877, p. 8 (marriage to Eva N. Carriger), known from a Newspapers.com clipping title; the clipping text has not been seen.
  • Tufts & Spieker, California Trade Mark No. 404, Yerba Santa Cough Balsam, 1877 (reported by a bottle collector’s blog; secondary).
  • Sacramento Historic Churches Index, 1852–1910, St. Paul’s Episcopal Church: Albert, Edith, Mrs. Eva Nelson Tufts, Jessie May Tufts (secondary index).
  • Edith Tufts Shaw, b. 7 Oct. 1878, Sacramento (index derived from Social Security records; secondary).

⚑ Unverified

Albert’s death about 1898–99 is inferred from his absence in the 1899 directory; no death record has been found. His birthplace, burial and any tie to the Massachusetts Tufts family are unknown. The trademark gives 10th & K, which conflicts with the directories (10th & J).
