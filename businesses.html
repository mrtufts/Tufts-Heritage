Long before the college that bears the name, the Tufts family earned its living from the clay, water and roads of the Mystic valley: ferrying, brickmaking, tanning, milling, fishing, distilling, printing and, later, pharmacy. Each entry below names the family member, the trade, the place and the dates, quoting the printed page. Doubtful identifications are marked Unverified. See also the Mystic River and Homesteads.

The Charlestown–Malden Ferry

Tufts: Peter Tufts (the immigrant, 1617–1700) · Business: Ferry · Location: Mystic River, between Charlestown and Malden · Dates: 1646–47

“He kept the Ferry between Charlestown and Malden with his brother-in-law, Bridges, in 1646-7”

Edward C. Booth, “The Tufts Family in Somerville,” Historic Leaves (Somerville Historical Society), vol. 1 (1902–03), part 1, p. 21. Perseus

The ferry crossed the Mystic. Booth adds that Peter was the largest landholder in Malden when he died in 1700. See Peter Tufts the Immigrant.

⚑ Unverified

The ferry’s exact landing places and the length of his tenure. Booth found no earlier record of him than 1646–47.

Brick-yard Pasture, Medford

Tufts: Dr. Simon Tufts · Business: Brickmaking land · Location: North of Dr. Tufts’s house, Medford · Dates: Bought March 26, 1761

“At a later date, say 1750, bricks were made on land directly north of Dr. Tufts's house. … This land, called Brick-yard Pasture, was owned by Rev. Matthew Byles, of Boston, and sold by him to Dr. Simon Tufts, March 26, 1761.”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 355. Text (Perseus)

Dr. Tufts’s house stood at Medford Square (see Homesteads).

⚑ Unverified

Brooks records the purchase of the land, not that Dr. Tufts made bricks himself. By 1761 the elder Dr. Simon had died (1747), so the buyer was presumably the younger Dr. Simon (1726–1786). Not confirmed by deed.

The Fountain Yards

Tufts: William, Hutchinson and Benjamin Tufts · Business: Brickmaking · Location: Near the “Fountain House,” about eighty rods east of Gravelly Bridge, Medford (toward the Malden line) · Dates: Early; discontinued before 1855

“Messrs. William Tufts, Thomas Bradshaw, Hutchinson Tufts, Benjamin Tufts, and Sylvanus Blanchard were the manufacturers in that locality. These yards have been discontinued within our day.”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 356. Text (Perseus)

The Medford Historical Society places the Fountain Yards along Fulton Street from Forest Street to the Malden line. Medford Historical Society

The “Sodom” Brickyards

Tufts: Isaac, Jonathan and Ebenezer Tufts, brothers; later Seth Tufts and his son Seth · Business: Brickmaking · Location: South side of the Mystic, near the Middlesex Canal, south-south-east of Rock Hill (between Summer and George Streets, on Brickyard Lane) · Dates: 18th century to shortly before 1855

“As the familiar and improper sobriquet of Sodom was early given to that part of Medford which lies south of the river, the brick-yards, opened by the brothers Isaac, Jonathan, and Ebenezer Tufts, obtained the local name. After these gentlemen came Seth Tufts, who, with his son Seth, carried on the business till recently. These yards were situated near Middlesex Canal and the river, about south-south-east from Rock Hill.”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 356. Text (Perseus)

The street location is from the Medford Historical Society. Medford bricks were carried by lighter down the Mystic to Boston.

⚑ Unverified

Whether these brothers are the Revolutionary soldiers Isaac (36-10), Jonathan (36-8) and Ebenezer (36-7) of the same family number. The names and family grouping fit, but Brooks does not give their dates.

Tan-yard and leather works

Tufts: Nathan Tufts · Business: Tanning and butchering; leather manufacture · Location: Medford (corner south-east of Whitmore’s Bridge), later Charlestown · Dates: Before 1855

“The first tan-yard in Medford was on the corner lot south-east of Whitmore's Bridge. … It was last owned by Mr. Nathan Tufts and Mr. Jonathan Brooks, in company. When they sold it, Mr. Tufts moved to Charlestown, and became the most extensive manufacturer of leather in the State.”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 353. Text (Perseus)

“Nathan, the youngest son of Nathan, Sr., was also a resident of Charlestown after his boyhood, and was an extensive butcher and tanner there.”

Edward C. Booth, “The Tufts Family in Somerville,” Historic Leaves (Somerville Historical Society), vol. 1 (1902–03), part 2, p. 21. Perseus

Booth adds that he owned “the large farms around the Powder House and Walnut hill” (p. 21). See the Powder House.

⚑ Unverified

That Brooks’s tanner and Booth’s butcher and tanner are the same Nathan Tufts. The match is likely but not confirmed. His dates and the site of the Charlestown works are not yet found.

Sandy Bank tide mill, 1746

Tufts: Dr. Simon Tufts, “Esq., physician” (the elder, 1700–1747) · Business: Gristmill (landowner and party to the agreement) · Location: Gravelly Creek, Medford · Dates: Agreement of Feb. 20, 1746

“Articles of agreement were concluded, Feb. 20, 1746, between Richard Sprague, cooper, Samuel Page, yeoman, Simon Tufts, Esq., physician, … all of Medford, and owners of land”

Charles Brooks, History of the Town of Medford (1855), p. 394. Text (Perseus)

Brooks says Dr. Tufts and others bound themselves “never to obstruct the free flow of water to the mill” (p. 394).

Tufts: William Tufts and Edward Tufts (with Gershom Cutter) · Business: Saw and grist milling · Location: Medford Turnpike (now Mystic Avenue) · Dates: By 1848; sold to Gershom Cutter about 1845–48

“‘the owners of the saw and grist mills on the turnpike,’ William Tufts, Edward Tufts and Gershom Cutter”

Tide Mill Institute, “Medford, Massachusetts: Tufts-Cutter Mill,” compiled extracts. PDF

⚑ Unverified

This is a compilation; its original printed source and page are not yet confirmed. The compilation says Gershom Cutter bought “the Tufts mill” in 1845, but it names William and Edward Tufts as owners in the 1848 agreement. Which William and Edward these were is not known.

Mystic River fishing rights

Edmund Tufts, Printer

Tufts: Edmund Tufts, son of Joseph Tufts (1760–1819) · Business: Printing · Location: The Peter Tufts of Winter Hill homestead, Somerville · Dates: About 1842–1851 and after

“Edmund was intimately connected with the early history of this town, and his sign on the old house, ‘Edmund Tufts, Printer,’ is still remembered. For some years he did the printing for the new town of Somerville and its inhabitants, and we find his name on most of the early town reports. He issued a Somerville Directory in 1851, a pamphlet of thirty-two pages.”

Edward C. Booth, “The Tufts Family in Somerville,” Historic Leaves (Somerville Historical Society), vol. 1 (1902–03), part 2, p. 24. Perseus

The 1851 directory was reprinted in the same volume of Historic Leaves (p. 25). See Homesteads.

⚑ Unverified

His birth and death dates and the years of the press. Somerville was set off in 1842, which suggests the start date; that is an inference.

James W. Tufts, apothecary and soda-fountain maker

Tufts: James Walker Tufts (1835–1902), son of Leonard Tufts of Charlestown · Business: Pharmacy and drug stores; Arctic soda apparatus; silver-plated ware · Location: Somerville Avenue near the Bleachery; Medford; Boston (Washington and Hanover Streets) · Dates: 1856–1895

“Leonard, who lived in Charlestown, was the father of James W. Tufts, who was at one time an apothecary in Somerville avenue, near the Bleachery. Mr. Tufts has since become well-known as a manufacturer of soda-water apparatus.”

Edward C. Booth, “The Tufts Family in Somerville,” Historic Leaves (Somerville Historical Society), vol. 1 (1902–03), part 2, p. 24. Perseus

He was apprenticed at fifteen to Samuel Kidder and Company, druggists of Charlestown, and opened his own pharmacy in Somerville in 1856. He later had stores in Medford, Boston, Winchester and Woburn, then sold the stores to make his Arctic Soda Apparatus. In 1891 his firm merged into the American Soda Fountain Company, which he headed. He also built the Bunker Hill Terraces tenements in Charlestown (1890) and later founded Pinehurst, N.C. (NCpedia) From 1875 his firm also made silver-plated wares. (Smithsonian) Booth places him in the line of John Tufts (b. 1755) of the Peter and Oliver Tufts House.

⚑ Unverified

NCpedia gives his apprenticeship at fifteen; another account says sixteen.

Trades Searched, Not Yet Found

⚑ Unverified

Shipbuilding. Medford’s shipyards (1803–1873) were run by Magoun, Turner, Lapham, Stetson, Sprague and James, Waterman and Ewell, Curtis, Foster and others. No Tufts shipyard owner has been found yet in Brooks or the Old Ship Street district history. A Tufts may have worked as a carpenter or owned shares in a ship; this is unchecked. Silversmithing. No colonial Tufts silversmith has been found. The only Tufts silver found so far is James W. Tufts’s silver plate (from 1875). Taverns. None is yet documented for a Tufts.
