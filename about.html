About the Author

Shannon Tufts leads Tufts Lineage & Legacy, which documents the Tufts family across the generations. Its Revolutionary War chapter honors the 250th anniversary of American Independence.

His research follows the extended Tufts lineage. It takes in the Revolutionary soldiers of the family: Dr. Cotton Tufts, Zachariah, William, Samuel and Barnabas Tufts, the minutemen, and the brickmakers of Medford, Somerville and Charlestown, Massachusetts. It also covers Charles Tufts, benefactor of Tufts University.

He works from primary and printed sources. These include the Tufts Kinsmen volumes I and II (cited by title and page), DAR records, Find a Grave, Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, and town records. They are listed on Sources.

Shannon claims Peter Tufts of Winter Hill (b. 1728) as his sixth great-grandfather. He is proud to carry forward the name and spirit of Peter and Anne Adams Tufts. Their son Peter Tufts Jr. answered the Lexington alarm, marching from Medford with Capt. Isaac Hall’s company. Eight other men of the name assembled that morning in the same First Company of Minute Men. Medford later set their names on its 1925 monument: Daniel, Ebenezer, James Jr., Corporal John, Jonathan, Sergeant Isaac, Samuel, and Samuel 3d. (Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, vol. XVI (1907), pp. 132–139; City of Medford, Minute Men monument, 1925; see the Medford roster)

That same morning, on Milk Row, Samuel Tufts, a son of Peter Tufts of Milk Row, was running bullets in a small hut behind the house and did not hear the Regulars pass. Widow Elizabeth Rand is said to have run, in her night-clothes, to warn him. He saddled his horse and rode across his farm to Cambridge with the news. That Samuel, born in 1737, was a brother of Peter of Winter Hill, and so Shannon’s sixth great-granduncle. (Ellen Chase, The Beginnings of the American Revolution, vol. II (1910), p. 355; Larkin T. Tufts and Edward C. Booth, “Tufts Genealogy—Earlier Generations,” NEHGR 51 (July 1897), pp. 304–305; Wyman, The Genealogies and Estates of Charlestown, vol. II (1879), p. 964)

⚑ Unverified

The Medford monument lists “Tufts John Corporal.” A profile of Cpl. John Tufts (1754–1839), a son of Peter of Winter Hill, notes a possible match; no record of his service has yet been matched in Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors, and Chase records that he was charged to stay behind that morning. The precise relationship of Daniel, Ebenezer, James Jr., Jonathan, Sergeant Isaac, Samuel, and Samuel 3d to Shannon has not been established from the sources cited on their profiles. Chase does not give the dates or parents of the Samuel Tufts whom Widow Rand warned; he is identified here as Samuel of Milk Row (b. 1737), brother of Peter of Winter Hill, from the house later known as General Greene’s headquarters.

On the name: the sources agree that Peter of Winter Hill married Anne Adams on April 19, 1750 (Brooks, History of Medford, 1855, p. 547; Booth, Historic Leaves, vol. 1, p. 21). His brother Timothy Tufts also married an Anne Adams, “a niece of the wife of his brothers” (Booth, p. 25). The two are different women.

He is based in Clearwater and Largo, Pinellas County, Florida.
